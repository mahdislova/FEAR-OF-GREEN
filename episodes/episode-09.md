# Episode 9 — The Break-out

**Cold open:** 1873, the offspring's side. Jamie's room first.

**In short:** Nixon kills Ada to protect the break-out, frees Marsh from Newgate and storms the Works with Nathaniel and Alice. Eldgard confesses. The Duke ends the Hardings in law and humiliates Hector in front of the clerks. Hector, feeling the heir's chair slip, orders his sister killed. Tamara kills him first. Marsh dies holding the wheel; Byron comes back for the boy he raised.

**Change:** the truth is spoken aloud.

**Foreground:** Nixon: Ada's death, Marsh, storming the Works, "Did you love me?"

**Background:** The Lost One and Morgana (reveal #2); Eldgard's confession; the Duke's warrant; Nathaniel's unanswered question; Marsh dies at the wheel; Byron comes back for the boy he raised; Tamara covers Ada, then outplays her brother.

**Not in this episode:** Josephine, Grise, the chief engineer.

- **Opening:** the servants' stair. Ada is on her way to report Nixon. The lamp smashes. After Ada dies, Nixon rights the lamp out of the habit of saving her a job. He has promised to carve on Sunday. For a few seconds he stands there with the lamp in his hands.
- At Newgate Marsh tells Nixon he is his uncle. As a Hargrave servant boy he entered the Works by freight and helped close its draw every cleaning day. He can get them in and shut down the extraction. Nixon breaks him out with Ada’s blood on his cuffs.
- Nathaniel, Alice and Nixon storm the Works.
- **Imprint reveal #2:** Morgana tells the Lost One she did what she was told: both boys, both seeds. The Lost One's crisis: was her love for Alice a plan as well?
- Eldgard's confession. **He makes the full argument, with numbers:** how many die in one winter without the light, and which wards and pumps go first. It should sound right, because it partly is.
- Then Nixon: "Did you love me, or did you keep me?" Eldgard: "Yes."
- **The Duke's warrant, in person.** The Duke comes to the Works gate himself. A Privy Council warrant suspends the Harding Charter; the Royal Engineers will enter the vaults at dusk. Hector has come to claim the Crown's emergency coal. In front of the clerks and the engineers the Duke gives it to Tamara instead, because the East's carts already run on her name. Ep 4's question comes back to Hector: the Duke asks his own in public, and Hector has no answer. (The Duke's track: his word in Ep 2, broken in Ep 4, the ultimatum in Ep 6, the warrant in Ep 9.)
- **Why Eldgard talks instead of seizing them.** Alistair's lock answers one mind, and Eldgard put that mind in Nathaniel in 1873. The transfer needs the lock turned. He comes alone because he needs his son willing. His numbers are his case for why Nathaniel should turn it. In Ep 10 Nathaniel does turn it, the other way.
- **Alice's lie, out loud.** When the Lost One asks whether her love was a plan, Alice answers the way she's always loved: by holding on. "I don't care whose plan it was. You're mine." It's the wrong answer, and Ep 10 is where she learns the right one.
- **Nathaniel's question, unanswered.** After "Yes", Nathaniel asks his father whether he ever saw a son or only the lock. Eldgard opens his mouth, and Morgana's palm hits the glass. The shriek, the lamps bursting, the fracture. Eldgard turns to the pod, and the answer never comes. In Ep 10 he will never be able to give it.
- **Marsh dies at the wheel.** Eldgard orders the draw restarted. Two core guard go to the draw room. Marsh puts his arm through the spokes and won't come off. They kill him, and his body jams the wheel; the draw stays stopped. The kept Hargrave holds the Hardings' machine still with his own weight, wearing the spectacles Ada sent him (Eps 4–5). Nobody sees him die.
- **Byron comes back.** Through the old core guard's stair, in his thirty-year-old guard's coat, Byron puts a guard into the wall before his blade reaches Alice. Half the guard he trained. "I said nothing for sixteen years. I'm done saying nothing." Enough of them stand down. The butler who chose the boys over the master (Ep 6) finishes the choice.
- **The plot twist: Hector orders his sister killed.** The Duke humiliates him at the Works gate and gives the Crown's coal to Tamara. Then a runner: their dying father has heard, and has sent for his lawyer, and for Tamara, alone, tonight. Hector feels the chair he was promised since birth go out from under him, and crosses the line: he orders Ajax to take her out. That afternoon Ajax brings word that it's done. Hector asks for proof. Ajax takes him to the Gin Lane crater, and someone puts a blade in Hector's back. It's Tamara, simply returning the favour. "Ajax— why?" "Everyone has a price, sir. Sorry." Ajax's price was paid long ago: she wrote his dead men's names (Ep 3), and the carts have run on her orders since Ep 7, when he took the first one east. She has known about the audit since Ep 7. Her monologue: she looked up to her big brother; she would have stood at his shoulder for ever; "I would have lived in peace, brother. You brought war to my doorstep."

## Scenes

### 1. The elder first (cold open)

1873, the offspring's side. Harding House at night, seen from inside something that has no body: pipes, warm walls, sleeping heartbeats. Its mother's voice is in it, very faint, from far below the city. *The elder first.* It finds a door with a nine-year-old behind it. Jamie Harding is awake, reading by a candle he isn't supposed to have. He looks up at the cold coming under the door, and he isn't afraid.

### 2. The servants' stair

Before dawn. Nixon is halfway down the servants' stair with the transfer papers in his coat when he hears a step above him, and sees lamplight turning toward Eldgard's rooms. Ada read the destination upside down. He goes back up two at a time and catches her on the landing. He takes her arm to stop her. She pulls. The lamp smashes. She goes down the stairs, all of them, and lies still on the flags.

Nixon goes down after her. Out of the habit of saving her a job, he rights the lamp. He has promised to carve on Sunday. For a few seconds he stands there with the lamp in his hands and her blood on his cuffs.

Tamara is at the top of the stair. She understands in one look. She comes down, takes the lamp out of his hands and sends the footman away. To the household she says Mrs Hargrave fell. "Tamara—" "Go. Whatever you're doing, go now."

### 3. The tower drops

Nixon reaches Marsh by the morning train. Through the cell door he asks if the old freight entrance still opens. “Where do you think they brought the dirt out?” Marsh used to go in with the empty wagons. Each cleaning day he stood ankle-deep in green silt waiting for the wheel that stopped the draw. Nathaniel asked for a way in. Here is a man who can also stop the machine. Only after Nixon opens the door does Marsh tell him whose brother he is.

The relief order catches them in the glazed transfer tower. The gaoler sends the entire occupied tower dropping past the galleries to its lock bay. Through the glass Nixon sees a different tier of astonished faces at every floor. He throws a tether around a falling orderly and hauls him inside. That costs them the landing. They come out at the bottom on the condemned moving stair.


### 4. Jennie gives her name again

After the first prison train leaves, Jennie returns to the district Guard desk. She asks to give a statement about Blackwood Bridge. She saw Josephine Harding put Miriam Bounds over the rail. The clerk recognizes Jennie’s signature and fetches her earlier statement from the laundry inquiry. She gave Miriam’s name there too, days after the girl went into the river. Which account does she want to withdraw?

“Both happened.” Jennie waits while he writes down what she saw from the stair. This time she stays to hear him read it back. She signs beneath Josephine’s name.


### 5. The hill face

The stair shudders under Marsh. He lifts his foot on the familiar beat. Nixon misses it. A flight of steps folds flat and the two men slide out onto the exposed freight terrace. Above them an entire patrol clears the parapet on leap-boosters. For one glorious second the guards hang against the snow. Then the chase is on.

A Lantern’s crystal bomb blooms into a cage of green facets across the terrace. A second catches the front of an empty ore wagon. Nixon takes Marsh onto the back as the boosters streak level with them. Ahead, the rails end over the chalk drop. Nixon boosts across to a patrol wing hanging in its cliff cradle. For a moment Marsh thinks his nephew has left him. Then Nixon comes back *up* beneath the falling wagon, wing spread from wrist to wrist, and Marsh drops into the harness against his chest. The wagon falls away. Seen from across the valley, Newgate is a whole hill of lit windows at the edge of London, with two men flying out of its shadow.


### 6. The warrant

The Works gate, mid-morning. The Duke arrives in person, with an officer of the Royal Engineers and a clerk carrying a dispatch box. He doesn't go inside. Eldgard comes out to him.

The Duke reads the warrant aloud where the gatemen can hear: by order of the Privy Council the Harding Charter is suspended, and the Royal Engineers will enter the vaults at dusk. He holds it out. Eldgard looks at his oldest friend's signature for a long time before he takes it.

Hector's carriage has drawn up behind. He has come for the Crown's emergency coal contract; there's no other coal. Tamara steps down after him. The clerk opens the box. The Duke takes out the contract and hands it past Hector, to his sister.

"Miss Lambrick has fed the East for a week on her own name, while the rest of us were pricing it."

Hector laughs. "My sister signs nothing without me. I speak for the house."

"You asked me a question in front of the Lords, Lambrick. Here's mine, in front of the clerks. Whose name is on the East's carts?"

Hector has no answer. Everyone at the gate knows it. Tamara signs on the clerk's box. She doesn't look at her brother. Eldgard, warrant in hand, goes back inside to the vaults. "Bring the child down. We finish before dusk."

### 7. Take her out

Hector's carriage. Ajax rides opposite, as he has for eleven years. Hector watches the Works go by and says nothing for a long time.

At the corner of Oxford Street a Lambrick runner jumps onto the step and speaks through the window. Lord Lambrick has heard what happened at the gate. He has sent for his lawyer. And for Miss Tamara, tonight. Alone.

Hector gives the boy a coin. The window goes up. He sits back.

"Thirty-four years," he says, to nobody. "Every morning of my life he told me whose chair it was."

"She's your sister, sir."

"She's a Harding now. She married them on Saturday. And tonight he's going to give her my chair in front of a lawyer." The Crown's contract is in her name, and a dead woman's contracts come back to the house, and the house comes to the heir. He names a price. "Take her out. Today. Somewhere the East gets the blame."

Ajax looks at him for a long moment. He has carried Hector's coat for eleven years. He saw Tamara spell his dead men's names. "Today," he agrees.

### 8. The boy’s door

They land beside the valley freight line and ride the returning empties to Tyburn. Nathaniel and Alice are waiting at the terminus. Marsh finds his old height mark behind the weigh clerk’s hatch. His hand remembers the catch above it. He takes them through to the draw room and winds the cleaning wheel until the green silt settles. The extraction stops. The reserve lamps come on. They have bought time to reach the captive child. Opening Morgana’s pod still takes Nathaniel and the Lost One. The assault, imprint and Eldgard confrontation follow in this episode. 


### 9. The pod chamber

The silt gallery runs under the draw-room floor toward the pod. Marsh knows it in the dark; he cleaned it as a boy. He takes them as far as the grate and goes back to hold the wheel: if it turns again, the draw starts. They come up through a grate into the chamber. Morgana hangs in her pod, failing. Beside it, strapped in the second set of restraints, is the Lost One. The core guard are on the gallery above. Nixon puts himself between them and the others.

### 10. Both boys, both seeds

With the draw stopped, Morgana is strong enough to speak. Her voice arrives in every head in the room, each in its own language. The Lost One did what she was told: both boys, both seeds. The elder to resist, the younger to understand the lock. And the woman on the bridge?

The Lost One looks at Alice. "Did I love you because I was made to?"

Alice is sawing at the straps. "I don't care whose plan it was. You're mine." The girl watches her hands, and doesn't answer.

Nathaniel finds the lock. The knowledge from 1873 knows exactly how it turns. His right hand doesn't. He tries left-handed and can't hold the pressure.

### 11. Yes

The gallery door opens. Eldgard comes in alone, unarmed, with the night ledger under his arm. He tells the guard to stay where they are. He hasn't come to take his son. He's come to ask him.

Alistair built the lock to answer one mind. "I put that mind in you, Nat. I'm sorry for how. The transfer needs the lock turned, and only you can turn it." Then he makes his case, and he makes it with numbers: how many die in one winter without the light, and which go first. The children's ward at Saint Bartholomew's on candles inside four hours. The Shadwell pumping station stopping, and the river cellars flooding. Every figure is true. It should sound right, because it partly is.

Nixon steps into the light with Ada's blood on his cuffs. "Did you love me, or did you keep me?"

Eldgard looks at him. "Yes."

Nathaniel gets up from the lock. He has spent his whole life waiting to be asked into this room, and now he's in it, and his father is asking him for something. It should feel like being chosen. It feels like being picked up by the handle.

"And me? When you looked at me, all those years at the end of the table. Did you ever see a son? Or did you just see the lock?"

Eldgard opens his mouth. For once in his life he looks like a man about to tell the truth.

Behind them, Morgana's palm hits the glass.

The sound comes through every head in the chamber at once, a shriek with words folded inside it. The lamps along the gallery flare white and burst. A hairline fracture runs across the pane, and keeps running. Eldgard turns to the pod, and whatever he was going to say to his son goes with him.

"Restart the draw," he tells the guard. "Now. Before she's strong enough to finish that."

### 12. The wheel

Two of the core guard go down the gallery stair toward the draw room. The rest come down into the chamber.

The draw room. Marsh is on the cleaning wheel, both hands on the spokes, the way he stood every cleaning day for twenty years in a servant boy's apron. He hears boots on the iron and doesn't turn round.

The first guard tells him to step away from the wheel. Marsh puts his arm through the spokes up to the shoulder. "I've stood here before you were born, son. I'll stand here after."

They try to pull him off. He doesn't come. The second guard takes out his quench-blade. Short and ugly. Once. Twice. Marsh sags against the wheel, and the wheel moves a quarter-inch, and stops, because his arm is still through the spokes and his weight is on it. They try to turn it with him on it. It won't turn. He dies there, the kept Hargrave, holding the Hardings' machine still with his own body. His spectacles are still on, the ones Ada sent him, one arm mended with thread.

The chamber. The guard are on them. Nixon takes the first on the stair and the second takes Nixon's legs out. Alice has her knife out of the straps and in the third man's forearm. The fourth gets behind her. His blade comes up.

A door nobody looked at opens in the chamber wall: the old core guard's stair from the Works yard, the one Byron climbed every night of his years on the guard. A man in a faded guard's coat thirty years out of fashion comes through it, and he doesn't hurry. He takes the fourth man's wrist, turns it, and puts him into the wall. The guard on the floor look up and know him. Half of them he trained.

He has been on his way down since breakfast, when the household was told Mrs Hargrave fell and Master Nat hadn't come home. "Stand down, lads," Byron says. "You know me. You know what's in that tank. I sat on the landing the night the master swung his lantern over a five-year-old boy, and I said nothing for sixteen years. I'm done saying nothing."

They don't all stand down. Enough of them do. Byron puts himself between the rest and Alice, and holds out his hand to Nathaniel without looking round. "Master Nat. You'd best finish whatever it is you came to do."

Behind them the fracture keeps running across the glass.

### 13. It's done

Lambrick House, afternoon. Upstairs a door closes on their father's lawyer. Ajax comes into the study with snow on his shoulders. "It's done."

Hector doesn't look up from his ledger. "Show me."

"Gin Lane. The washhouse. The East will get the credit, like you said."

Hector puts on his coat himself. The left cuff is undone. He doesn't notice.

### 14. Gin Lane (tag)

The Gin Lane crater under grey afternoon light. Snow on the green glass where the washhouse stood. Ajax leads Hector across the rubble to a shape under a woman's cloak, Tamara's dark blue. Hector kneels and lifts the edge. Coal sacks.

The blade goes in under his shoulder blade. He turns on his knees. Tamara is standing behind him with her hands red to the wrist.

He looks past her. "Ajax— why?"

"Everyone has a price, sir. Sorry."

Tamara crouches so her brother can see her face. She takes his wrist, and does up the cuff he left undone, the way she did on her wedding morning.

"When I was six you taught me to ride, and you let me win the race home so Father would see. I thought you were the cleverest man in England. I learned to count from your columns. I'd have stood at your shoulder for the rest of my life, Hector. I'd have found and you'd have said, and I'd have let you wear every move I ever made, and clapped. I never once asked for the chair. I asked you to let me live in the house." Her voice doesn't rise. "And this morning, because an old man sent for a lawyer, you sent Ajax to kill me."

She takes the audit out of her coat, the one from his drawer. *Gin Lane.* "You knew this street would go. You let it go so you could sell coal to whoever was left. I'm standing where you let them burn."

He tries to speak. She waits, politely, the way he taught her to wait in a negotiation. Nothing comes.

"I would have lived in peace, brother. You brought war to my doorstep." Very quietly: "You taught me what grief costs. I only paid it forward."

Hector Lambrick dies in the snow where the washhouse was, with his cuff done up. Tamara stands and takes her cloak back off the coal sacks. Ajax asks what story London should hear. "The East had every reason. Let them have the credit." She wipes her hands on the snow. "And Ajax. Father's sent for me tonight. Have the carriage ready."
