# Episode 9 — The Break-out

**Cold open:** 1873, the offspring's side. Jamie's room first.

**In short:** Nixon kills Ada to protect the break-out, frees Marsh from Newgate and storms the Works with Nathaniel and Alice. Eldgard confesses, and the one question his son asks goes unanswered. Across town the war for the Lambrick chair ends in the snow at Gin Lane.

**Change:** the truth is spoken aloud.

**Foreground:** Nixon: Ada's death, Marsh, storming the Works, "Did you love me?"

**Background:** The Lost One and Morgana (reveal #2); the Duke's warrant; Byron's return; Hector and Tamara.

**Not in this episode:** Josephine, Grise, the chief engineer.

**The spine.** Three lines run through one Sunday and cross at the Works gate:
- **The Hargraves.** The stair, Newgate, the boy's door and the wheel. Nixon kills one Hargrave at dawn and is too late for the other by afternoon.
- **The Hardings.** Reveal #2, Eldgard's numbers, "Yes", and the question Morgana's palm cuts off. Ep 10 makes it unanswerable for ever.
- **The Lambricks.** The Duke's warrant, the runner, the order and the blade. Tamara covers Nixon's killing at dawn and commits her own by dusk.

**The beats.**
- **Opening:** the servants' stair. Ada is on her way to report Nixon. The lamp smashes. After Ada dies, Nixon rights the lamp out of the habit of saving her a job. He has promised to carve on Sunday.
- At Newgate Marsh tells Nixon he is his uncle. He can get them into the Works by freight and stop the draw. Nixon breaks him out with Ada's blood on his cuffs.
- **The Duke's warrant, in person:** the Charter suspended; Royal Engineers in the vaults at dusk. The Crown's coal goes to Tamara in front of her brother. "Whose name is on the East's carts?" (Ep 4's question, returned.) Their father's runner reaches the gate before the Duke's carriage has left it: Lord Lambrick has sent for his lawyer, and for Tamara, tonight. Hector orders the hit before he's home.
- **Imprint reveal #2:** Morgana tells the Lost One she did what she was told: both boys, both seeds. "Did I love you because I was made to?" Alice answers the old way: "You're mine." (Ep 10 corrects it.)
- **Eldgard's confession, with numbers.** He comes alone because the lock answers only the mind he put in Nathaniel; he needs his son willing. "Did you love me, or did you keep me?" "Yes." Then Nathaniel: "A son, or the lock?" Morgana's palm hits the glass before the answer.
- **Marsh dies at the wheel,** unseen, and jams it with his body. **Byron comes back** through the core guard's stair and talks half the guard down.
- **Tamara's track.**
  - *The servants' stair:* she finds Nixon over Ada and takes the lamp. "Go. Whatever you're doing, go now." Love: she saves him. Power: she holds his worst act for ever.
  - *The Works gate:* the Duke hands her the Crown's coal. She doesn't look at her brother.
  - *Gin Lane (tag):* the blade. Ajax's price was paid in Ep 3 (the names) and Ep 7 (the first cart).
- **Tag:** in the Gin Lane crater, Hector dies at his sister's feet, where he let a street burn. "I would have lived in peace, brother. You brought war to my doorstep."

## Scenes

### 1. The elder first (cold open)

1873, the offspring's side. Harding House at night, seen from inside something with no body: pipes, warm walls, sleeping heartbeats. Its mother's voice is in it, faint, from far below the city. *The elder first.* A door with a nine-year-old behind it. Jamie Harding is awake, reading by a candle he isn't supposed to have. He looks up at the cold coming under the door, and he isn't afraid.

### 2. The servants' stair

Before dawn. Nixon is halfway down the servants' stair with the transfer papers in his coat when he sees lamplight above him, turning toward Eldgard's rooms. Ada read the destination upside down. He goes back up two at a time and catches her on the landing. He takes her arm. She pulls. The lamp smashes. She goes down the stairs, all of them, and lies still on the flags.

He goes down after her. Out of the habit of saving her a job, he rights the lamp. He has promised to carve on Sunday. He stands there with the lamp in his hands and her blood on his cuffs.

Tamara is at the top of the stair. She understands in one look. She comes down, takes the lamp out of his hands and sends the footman away. To the household she says Mrs Hargrave fell. "Tamara—" "Go. Whatever you're doing, go now."

### 3. The tower drops

Nixon reaches Marsh by the morning train. Through the cell door he asks if the old freight entrance still opens. “Where do you think they brought the dirt out?” Marsh used to go in with the empty wagons. Each cleaning day he stood ankle-deep in green silt waiting for the wheel that stopped the draw. Nathaniel asked for a way in. Here is a man who can also stop the machine. Only after Nixon opens the door does Marsh tell him whose brother he is.

The relief order catches them in the glazed transfer tower. The gaoler sends the entire occupied tower dropping past the galleries to its lock bay. Through the glass Nixon sees a different tier of astonished faces at every floor. He throws a tether around a falling orderly and hauls him inside. That costs them the landing. They come out at the bottom on the condemned moving stair.


### 4. Jennie gives her name again

After the first prison train leaves, Jennie returns to the district Guard desk. She asks to give a statement about Blackwood Bridge. She saw Josephine Harding put Miriam Bounds over the rail. The clerk recognizes Jennie’s signature and fetches her earlier statement from the laundry inquiry. She gave Miriam’s name there too, days after the girl went into the river. Which account does she want to withdraw?

“Both happened.” Jennie waits while he writes down what she saw from the stair. This time she stays to hear him read it back. She signs beneath Josephine’s name.


### 5. The hill face

The stair shudders under Marsh. He lifts his foot on the familiar beat. Nixon misses it. A flight of steps folds flat and the two men slide out onto the exposed freight terrace. Above them an entire patrol clears the parapet on leap-boosters. For one glorious second the guards hang against the snow. Then the chase is on.

A Lantern’s crystal bomb blooms into a cage of green facets across the terrace. A second catches the front of an empty ore wagon. Nixon takes Marsh onto the back as the boosters streak level with them. Ahead, the rails end over the chalk drop. Nixon boosts across to a patrol wing hanging in its cliff cradle. For a moment Marsh thinks his nephew has left him. Then Nixon comes back *up* beneath the falling wagon, wing spread from wrist to wrist, and Marsh drops into the harness against his chest. The wagon falls away. Seen from across the valley, Newgate is a whole hill of lit windows at the edge of London, with two men flying out of its shadow.


### 6. The warrant

The Works gate, mid-morning. The Duke comes in person, with an officer of the Royal Engineers and a clerk carrying a dispatch box. He doesn't go inside. Eldgard comes out to him.

The Duke reads the warrant where the gatemen can hear: the Harding Charter is suspended, and the Royal Engineers will enter the vaults at dusk. Eldgard looks at his oldest friend's signature for a long time before he takes it.

Hector's carriage has drawn up behind; he has come for the Crown's emergency coal, because there is no other coal. Tamara steps down after him. The clerk opens the box. The Duke hands the contract past Hector, to his sister.

"Miss Lambrick has fed the East for a week on her own name, while the rest of us were pricing it."

Hector laughs. "My sister signs nothing without me. I speak for the house."

"You asked me a question in front of the Lords, Lambrick. Here's mine, in front of the clerks. Whose name is on the East's carts?"

Hector has no answer, and everyone at the gate knows it. Tamara signs on the clerk's box. She doesn't look at her brother. Eldgard goes back inside with the warrant. "We finish before dusk."

The Duke's carriage hasn't reached the corner when a Lambrick runner comes up the street at a dead run, and stops at the sight of them both. He gives the message to the air between them, because he doesn't know which of them to give it to. Lord Lambrick has heard what happened at the gate. He has sent for his lawyer. And for Miss Tamara. Tonight. Alone.

Tamara thanks the boy. Hector gets back into his carriage without a word. Ajax climbs in after him.

Inside, Hector stares at the Works going by. "Thirty-four years. Every morning of my life he told me whose chair it was." Then, flatly: "She's a Harding now. A dead woman's contracts come back to the house, and the house comes to me." He names a price. "Take her out. Today. Somewhere the East gets the blame."

Ajax has carried Hector's coat for eleven years. He watched Tamara spell his dead men's names. "Today," he says.

### 7. The boy’s door

They land beside the valley freight line and ride the returning empties to Tyburn. Nathaniel and Alice are waiting at the terminus. Marsh finds his old height mark behind the weigh clerk’s hatch. His hand remembers the catch above it. He takes them through to the draw room and winds the cleaning wheel until the green silt settles. The extraction stops. The reserve lamps come on. They have bought time to reach the captive child. Opening Morgana’s pod still takes Nathaniel and the Lost One. The assault, imprint and Eldgard confrontation follow in this episode. 


### 8. Both boys, both seeds

The silt gallery runs under the draw-room floor toward the pod. Marsh knows it in the dark; he cleaned it as a boy. At the grate he stops and goes back to hold the wheel: if it turns again, the draw starts. The others come up into the chamber.

Morgana hangs in her pod, failing. Beside it, in the second set of restraints, is the Lost One. The core guard line the gallery above. Nixon puts himself between them and the others.

With the draw stopped, Morgana is strong enough to speak. Her voice arrives in every head in the room, each in its own language. Her child did what she was told: both boys, both seeds. The elder to resist, the younger to understand the lock. And the woman on the bridge?

The Lost One looks at Alice. "Did I love you because I was made to?"

Alice is sawing at the straps. "I don't care whose plan it was. You're mine." The girl watches Alice's hands, and doesn't answer.

### 9. Yes

The gallery door opens. Eldgard comes in alone and unarmed, the night ledger under his arm. He tells the guard to stay where they are. He hasn't come to take his son. He's come to ask him.

"Alistair built the lock to answer one mind. I put that mind in you, Nat. I'm sorry for how. The transfer needs the lock turned, and only you can turn it." Then he makes his case with numbers: how many die in one winter without the light, and which go first. The children's ward at Saint Bartholomew's on candles inside four hours. The Shadwell pumps stopping, and the river cellars flooding. Every figure is true. It should sound right, because it partly is.

Nixon steps into the light with Ada's blood on his cuffs. "Did you love me, or did you keep me?"

Eldgard looks at him. "Yes."

Nathaniel has spent his whole life waiting to be asked into this room. Now his father is asking him for something, and it doesn't feel like being chosen. It feels like being picked up by the handle.

"And me? All those years at the end of the table. Did you ever see a son? Or did you just see the lock?"

Eldgard opens his mouth. For once in his life he looks like a man about to tell the truth.

Morgana's palm hits the glass.

The sound comes through every head at once, a shriek with words folded inside it. The gallery lamps flare white and burst. A hairline fracture runs across the pane, and keeps running. Eldgard turns to the pod, and whatever he was going to say goes with him.

"Restart the draw. Now. Before she's strong enough to finish that."

### 10. The wheel

Two of the guard go down toward the draw room. The rest come down into the chamber.

The draw room. Marsh is on the cleaning wheel, both hands on the spokes, the way he stood every cleaning day for twenty years in a servant boy's apron. He hears boots on the iron and doesn't turn round. When they tell him to step away, he puts his arm through the spokes up to the shoulder. "I've stood here before you were born, son. I'll stand here after."

They try to pull him off. He doesn't come. A quench-blade. Once. Twice. The wheel moves a quarter-inch under his weight, and stops. They try to turn it with him on it. It won't turn. He dies there, holding the Hardings' machine still with his own body, in the spectacles Ada sent him.

The chamber. Nixon takes the first guard on the stair, and the second takes Nixon's legs out. Alice's knife comes out of the straps and goes into the third man's forearm. The fourth is behind her. His blade comes up.

A door nobody looked at opens in the chamber wall: the old core guard's stair from the Works yard. A man in a guard's coat thirty years out of fashion comes through it, and he doesn't hurry. He takes the fourth man's wrist, turns it, and puts him into the wall. The guard on the floor look up and know him. Half of them he trained.

He has been on his way down since breakfast, when the household was told Mrs Hargrave fell and Master Nat hadn't come home. "Stand down, lads," Byron says. "You know what's in that tank. I sat on the landing the night the master swung his lantern over a five-year-old boy, and I said nothing for sixteen years. I'm done saying nothing."

Not all of them stand down. Enough do. Byron puts himself between the rest and Alice, and holds his hand out to Nathaniel without looking round. "Master Nat. Finish whatever you came to do."

Behind them the fracture keeps running across the glass.

### 11. Gin Lane (tag)

Lambrick House, afternoon. Upstairs a door closes on their father's lawyer. Ajax comes into the study with snow on his shoulders. "It's done." Hector doesn't look up. "Show me." He puts on his coat himself. The left cuff is undone. He doesn't notice.

The Gin Lane crater under grey light. Snow on the green glass where the washhouse stood. Ajax leads him across the rubble to a shape under a woman's cloak, Tamara's dark blue. Hector kneels and lifts the edge. Coal sacks.

The blade goes in under his shoulder blade. He turns on his knees. Tamara is standing behind him, red to the wrist.

He looks past her. "Ajax— why?"

"Everyone has a price, sir. Sorry."

Tamara crouches so her brother can see her face. She takes his wrist and does up the cuff he left undone, the way she did on her wedding morning.

"When I was six you taught me to ride, and you let me win the race home so Father would see. I learned to count from your columns. I'd have stood at your shoulder for the rest of my life, Hector. I'd have found and you'd have said, and I'd have clapped. I never once asked for the chair. I asked to live in the house." Her voice doesn't rise. "And this morning, because an old man sent for a lawyer, you sent Ajax to kill me."

She takes the audit out of her coat. *Gin Lane.* "You knew this street would go. You let it go so you could sell coal to whoever was left. I'm standing where you let them burn."

He tries to speak. She waits, politely, the way he taught her to wait in a negotiation. Nothing comes.

"I would have lived in peace, brother. You brought war to my doorstep." Very quietly: "You taught me what grief costs. I only paid it forward."

Hector Lambrick dies in the snow where the washhouse was, with his cuff done up. Tamara takes her cloak back off the coal sacks. Ajax asks what London should hear. "The East had every reason. Let them have the credit." She wipes her hands on the snow. "Father's sent for me tonight. Have the carriage ready."
