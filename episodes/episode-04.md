# Episode 4 — The Hearing

**Cold open:** The deal. Morgana teaches; Alistair: "You have my word."

**In short:** Lambrick takes the light's failure to Parliament and breaks the Duke in public. Grise tests "Miriam". Eldgard decides that when the mother dies, they use the child.

**Change:** the light's failure goes public.

**Foreground:** The hearing: Lambrick's attack, the Duke broken, Tamara's move.

**Background:** Nathaniel and Alice match the shard; Grise's test; Nixon at Newgate.

**Not in this episode:** Byron.

- Lambrick attacks the Harding Charter in Parliament with yield figures smuggled out by the Works' chief engineer. The lamps flicker in the chamber on cue.
- **The Duke keeps his word** and tells the Lords it's a fault in the lines. Hector asks him one question: "Your Grace, how many days did you tell your sister to lay in?" The Hardings' most respected witness is broken in public, and so is a friendship. Afterwards, in the robing room, the Duke tells Eldgard not to ask him again: "Ask me when the lamps in my sister's street go out."
- Tamara at the hearing: the codebreaker, the engagement as a merger on both sides.
- The prison train climbs to Newgate on its great chalk hill at the city's edge, the cell galleries cut into the hill's face above the freight line. From the glazed transfer tower Nixon sees relief guards glide down to the valley on folding patrol wings. The moving stair sticks on the way to Marsh’s landing. Every prisoner lifts a foot at the same instant. Nixon visits Marsh in Newgate. The madman pinches his chin and whispers Hargrave. Nixon walks away not understanding. When he mentions the encounter to Ada, she asks if Marsh has his spectacles. Nixon tries to learn how she knows him. She wraps the glasses and explains that one arm is loose. Nixon must ask for the cell to be opened and give them to Marsh himself. He agrees. Ada asks whether he is coming on Sunday. He asks if she still keeps the crisp end of the joint for him. She tells him he can cut it himself this time. He promises to come. Ada has found a way to care for her brother in the very prison she helped put him in.
- **Why Nixon goes to Newgate:** the Frost Killer is his case, and the frost "like gallowglass" sends him to the Newgate register for anyone ever held over green frost. One name: Tobias Marsh, committed for raving about frost under Tyburn.
- Nathaniel and Alice match the shard to a Works drawing. Jamie could have opened the ornament himself. The Guard discounted that possibility on the strength of Nathaniel's statement. His silence now protects his own lie. Alone, he says it aloud for the first time: Jamie let it out. Why? At the workshop Nathaniel shows Miriam the exterior drawing and asks whether Jamie showed her the ornament. She asks for time. He leaves the drawing with her. The romance warms; Alice reports to Josephine and hates it. As they talk Alice fastens the pearl buttons at Josephine's wrist. One button has left a blue mark on the linen. Josephine rubs at it. Alice says cold water will take it out. Jennie keeps a room over the old dyehouse, and whenever the stair leaks they have to wash that same blue out of their hems. Josephine fetches a little water and lets Alice finish the cuff. Josephine offers the wrist on purpose and holds perfectly still: she is letting her victim's sister touch her. A confession nobody hears.
- **Grise tests "Miriam".** Grise knows from the street that Alice has been playing her (Ep 3), and she holds the one thing Alice can't afford to lose: the contract. Through Jennie she sends Alice an ultimatum. Bring your sister in to report, or Grise gives Ajax her address. Grise sells to everyone; Alice believes her. Walking Miriam into Grise's room, on Alice's own terms, buys time. Alice sets the terms: daylight, the laundry floor full of women, and she doesn't leave her sister's side. Grise asks for a Vykra report and gets fragments and nonsense. Then she gives a Vykra hand-sign the real Miriam would answer without thinking. The Lost One doesn't. Alice is too new to know what the sign meant. Grise smiles: it isn't Miriam, and it's worth more than anything she's ever sold. She orders Jennie to follow the sisters home. Jennie comes back and says she lost them in the fog. It's her first lie to Grise. Grise sells to Lambrick anyway: a sighting, and the girl who walked them out. The betrayal Alice should have expected from a woman who sells to everyone.
- **The fog (Alice's first aura moment).** On the way home four bounty men, after the reward on the Frost Killer's bulletin, close on the sisters in a Shadwell alley. Alice puts all four on the cobbles in seconds. Jennie, following on Grise's orders, sees it from the corner. It's why she lies.
- **The audit goes home.** With the sighting, Grise sells Hector back the audit Miriam stole in Ep 1. Hector locks it in his desk and says nothing about what it proves.
- **Tamara's track.**
  - *Lambrick House, breakfast:* she breaks the engineer's cipher in three lines; Hector takes the figures to Parliament.
  - *Westminster, the gallery:* she slips Hector a card: *Ask the Duke how many days.* He breaks the Duke, and the chamber applauds Hector. She sits with her hands in her lap. The last time she lets him wear her move.
- **Tag:** Eldgard to Josephine: when the mother dies, they use the child. The audience knows the stakes before the characters do.

## Scenes

### 1. The deal (cold open)

c. 1780. The cellar under the old Harding house, lit by one lantern. Morgana sits chained to a beam, so thin her bones show blue through the skin. On the frosted wall beside her she draws with one fingertip: a coil, a spiral, the heart of a machine. Alistair copies every line into a book.

She stops drawing. "And then you let us go. Me and my child." He closes the book on his pencil. "You have my word." She goes back to drawing.

### 2. Three lines

Lambrick House, breakfast. Hector frowns over the yield sheets the engineer smuggled out in Harding cipher. Tamara reaches across, pulls the sheets to her side of the toast rack and breaks the cipher in three lines. Output down by a third. The core running hot. Hector folds the figures into his coat. "Today the Hardings find out the lamps can be counted."

### 3. The shard

Nathaniel's workshop. He hasn't slept. He lays the shard from Jamie's study on a Works drawing of the ornament's housing, and it fits the clasp like a key in a lock. Alice leans over his shoulder. The edge isn't torn; it was turned by a tool. Nobody broke in through the window. Jamie opened it himself.

The Guard ruled that out because Nathaniel told Nixon nothing was disturbed. Every day he keeps quiet now, he's protecting his own lie. Alice puts her hand over his to stop it shaking. He lets it stay there longer than he means to, then takes it back.

When she goes to fetch water he says it out loud, to nobody. "He let it out. Why?"

He shows the girl the exterior drawing and asks whether Jamie ever showed her this. She looks at it a long time. "Time," she says. He leaves the drawing with her.

### 4. The register

Guard headquarters. The Frost Killer is Nixon's case. The frost at the market was green-white, "like gallowglass", and Nixon goes to the Newgate register for anyone ever held over green frost. One name in fifteen years: Tobias Marsh, committed for raving about frost under Tyburn.

### 5. Newgate

The prison train climbs the great chalk hill at the city's edge, the cell galleries cut into the face above the freight line. From the glazed transfer tower Nixon watches relief guards glide down to the valley on folding patrol wings. On the way to Marsh's landing the moving stair sticks. Every prisoner lifts a foot at the same instant. Nixon doesn't.

Marsh is old and filthy and can't see past his own hands. Nixon asks about frost under Tyburn. Marsh doesn't answer. He reaches through the bars, pinches Nixon's chin between finger and thumb, and turns his face to the light. "Hargrave," he whispers. Nixon walks away not understanding.

### 6. The spectacles

The Harding scullery. Nixon mentions the madman at Newgate. Ada asks whether he has his spectacles. Nixon asks how she knows him. She wraps a pair of wire glasses in brown paper and explains that one arm is loose. He's to ask for the cell to be opened and give them to Marsh himself. He agrees. She asks if he's coming on Sunday. He asks if she still keeps the crisp end of the joint for him. "You can cut it yourself this time." He promises.

### 7. A terrible message

The Bounds room, where Alice has come back from the workshop for clean clothes. Jennie arrives with a caraway loaf stolen from the laundry kitchen and lye on her face. Then she produces Grise's terms: bring your sister in to report by noon, or Ajax gets your address. Alice tells her she's a terrible messenger. "I'm a good messenger. It's a terrible message."

Grise knows this room. If Ajax watches it, sooner or later Alice leads him to the workshop. So Alice sets terms of her own for Jennie to carry back: daylight, the laundry floor full of women, and she doesn't leave her sister's side for a second.

### 8. The hand-sign

Noon at the Shadwell laundry. Steam, lye, every woman at the tubs. Grise asks for a Vykra report and gets fragments and nonsense in Miriam's voice. Alice answers for her where she can. Then Grise's hand moves on her knee: three fingers, a turn of the wrist. The real Miriam would have answered before she knew she'd seen it. The girl watches the hand with polite interest.

Alice doesn't know what the sign meant. Grise's eyes go to the girl's collarbone, where Miriam had a scar from fourteen, and find clean skin. She smiles. It isn't Miriam. It's worth more than anything she's ever sold. "Go home, girls." When they've gone she nods to Jennie. "Follow them."

### 9. The fog

A Shadwell alley, fog off the river. Four dockers step out of a doorway. One holds up the Guard's bulletin with the Frost Killer's description and the reward. They want the girl.

Alice puts her sister behind her. The first man reaches. She breaks his wrist. The second swings a cosh and she's inside it; his knee goes. The third gets a hand in her collar and goes face-first into the wall. The fourth runs. It's over before the streetlamp has finished flickering.

At the corner Jennie stands with her back to the bricks. She has seen what Alice is. She has seen who Alice is protecting.

### 10. The audit

The laundry, afternoon. Grise writes a note to Lambrick House: a sighting of the Frost Killer, and the name of the girl who walked her out. She wraps the note round Hector's audit, the one Miriam stole from the Narrows, and names a price for both. At Lambrick House Hector reads the audit through once, locks it in his desk and pays.

### 11. The hearing

Westminster. Lamps in bronze crystal collars line the chamber. Hector lays out the smuggled figures, and on cue the lamps dim. A chamber full of faces turns up toward them.

The Duke rises for the Hardings. A fault in the lines, temporary, being mended. He says it beautifully. In the gallery Tamara slips her father a card: *Ask the Duke how many days.*

"Your Grace, how many days did you tell your sister to lay in?"

The Duke looks up at Eldgard. Eldgard doesn't look back. The Duke has been an honest man for forty years, and he can't say a flat lie under oath for a friend who won't meet his eyes. He sits down. The chamber applauds Hector. Tamara sits with her hands in her lap.

### 12. The robing room

Eldgard finds the Duke unpinning his chain of office. "You said it for Jamie." "I said it in front of the Lords, and they'll remember it longer than Jamie." "Will you say it again, if I need you?" The Duke, very gently: "Ask me when the lamps in my sister's street go out." He leaves by the river door.

### 13. Two buttons

Harding House. Alice reports on Nathaniel's day and leaves out the shard. She fastens the pearl buttons at Josephine's wrist. One has left a blue mark on the linen. Josephine rubs at it. Cold water will take it out, Alice says. Jennie keeps a room over the old dyehouse, and whenever the stair leaks they have to wash that same blue out of their hems. Josephine fetches a little water and holds her wrist out again, perfectly still, while Alice finishes the cuff.

### 14. Lost them

The laundry at dusk. Jennie comes back from the fog. Grise asks where the sisters went. "Lost them in the fog." It's the first lie she has ever told Grise. Grise looks at her a long moment and goes back to her ledger.

### 15. Use the child (tag)

Eldgard's study, night. Josephine stands at the cold grate. The figures are public now. Eldgard tells her what the engineer told him: the mother won't survive the winter. "When the mother dies, we use the child."
