# Episode 3 — The Frost Killer

**Cold open:** The Essex marsh line, during Nixon's nine nights in the east. A Guard beacon gutters and dies. A young Guardsman's hands freeze to the iron housing. Nixon wraps his own scarf around them and looks out at a horizon where whole villages have gone dark.

**In short:** The girl's cold breaks out at the market and London names a Frost Killer. Nathaniel hides the sisters and sends Nixon's search to the wrong street.

**Change:** Nathaniel takes a side.

**Foreground:** The Frost Killer: the market outburst, the hunt, Nathaniel hiding the sisters.

**Background:** Alice playing Grise; Hector pays the widows; Eldgard names the monster; Nixon and Tamara.

**Not in this episode:** Josephine, Ada, Marsh, Byron, Morgana.

- Morning in the Bounds room. Still in disbelief, Jennie tells Alice to keep it a secret that Miriam is back. Alice asks her why. Jennie can't say, and leaves. Grise swore Miriam ran off and left her; Miriam is in Alice's bed. Grise lied.
- Alice leaves the girl with Ren and goes to the laundry alone. She plays the grieving initiate and takes her first envelope: Nathaniel Harding. "Any word of my sister?" "Still gone, love." Now Alice knows Grise is lying to her face, and Grise doesn't know she knows. She says nothing. She takes the job to stay inside long enough to learn what Grise had to do with the bridge.
- The girl slips away from Ren the way a child slips a sitter, pulled toward Alice. She finds her at the market, where Alice is stalking Nathaniel. Alice yelps, "Hey, what are you doing here?" The girl holds up her hand: "Food." At the market the girl follows a simple spoken instruction from Alice for the first time. Her first clear words come in the workshop tag.
- Before his shift Nixon meets Tamara privately. She embraces him the moment the door closes. He wants to walk her home openly. He asks whether his pay and his place in the household make him someone she can only see here. She loves him. The engagement letter is in her glove. When he asks about walking her home openly, her fingers go to it, and stay there. She asks for time to speak to Hector herself instead. She knows about the Harding arrangement and lets Nixon leave believing the timing is still theirs to choose.
- Lambrick's men attack Alice. The girl realises she has to protect her, and the cold breaks out of her: an ice outburst that kills several of Lambrick's men and some civilians, impaling bodies nine feet in the air. London names a Frost Killer, and blames Jamie's death on it.
- **Hector pays the widows.** At first light he stands in Gin Lane in the snow with Ajax and a strongbox and pays every family the ice touched, not only his own men's. The Hardings send no one. Tamara holds the ledger. "You're paying for the Hardings' monster." "I'm paying for the street's memory. In a month they'll need coal. They'll remember who came." "And if they ask why you came?" "Grief, my dear. It's the cheapest thing I'll ever buy."
- **Eldgard names the monster.** Nixon reports the market killings. Eldgard drafts the Guard's public statement himself on the family's paper: "The Frost Killer." "We don't know it's the same—" "The frost in my son's study. The frost at the market. Londoners need one name for what they're afraid of, Captain, or they'll find their own. Sign it as the officer in charge." Nixon signs: the first time his name goes under something he doesn't know is true. The name London uses is Eldgard's.
- Word reaches Grise from the street: Miriam Bounds is alive, and was at the market killing men with ice. Alice sat across from her that morning and said nothing. Grise realises Alice is playing her.
- Nixon opens the Christmas market gates and puts the Lantern Guard overhead. Alice buys the sisters a way out under the descending paper stars. Ajax hunts too, for Lambrick, who has heard the killer leaves frost like gallowglass.
- Nathaniel finds the sisters and hides them in his workshop. His tech can manage her cold.
- **The first lie to Nixon: the wrong street.** At the Guard post that night, Nixon gratefully shows Nathaniel the search map. Nathaniel reads it like an engineer and points the frost along the old drains toward Gin Lane. "That's where I'd look." Twenty men move to Gin Lane; Clerkenwell stays open. ## Scenes

### 1. The marsh line (cold open)

The Essex marsh, one of Nixon's nine nights in the east. Sleet sideways off the estuary. At a Guard outpost the beacon gutters, flares and goes out. A young Guardsman grabs the housing to steady it and his hands stay there. When he pulls, the skin stays too. Nixon tears off his scarf and binds the boy's hands. Behind them, where three villages should be glowing on the horizon, there is nothing at all.

### 2. Buns

Dawn in the Bounds room. The girl is asleep in Miriam's shift, with Miriam's red mittens still on. She wouldn't take them off last night, though she never seems to feel the cold. In her sleep she holds one against her face. Alice watches her do it and doesn't know why it frightens her. Jennie lets herself in with three buns in a dishcloth, puts them on the stove to warm and burns her thumb on the kettle. She blames the laundry steam, which isn't here. Alice laughs before she can help it.

Then Jennie looks at the bed, and her face goes. She takes Alice by both wrists. "Don't tell Grise she's back. Don't let anyone in Shadwell see her." Alice asks why. Jennie opens her mouth, shuts it, and leaves her bun on the stove. Alice looks at the sleeping girl. Grise swore Miriam ran off and left her. Grise lied.

### 3. Still gone

The Shadwell laundry. Alice comes in with her hair pinned flat, the grieving initiate. Grise gives her condolences and her first envelope: Nathaniel Harding. Where he goes, who he sees, and keep him away from the river.

"Any word of my sister?"

"Still gone, love."

Alice thanks her. She counts the coins in the envelope on her way out, as a new girl would.

### 4. The coat

A rented room above a cooper's yard, before Nixon's shift. Tamara has her arms round his neck before the latch has clicked. He wants to walk her home on Sunday where people can see. He asks if his pay and his place below stairs make him someone she can only meet here. She loves him. The engagement letter is in her glove. Her fingers go to it and stay there.

Instead she gives him his Guard greatcoat, relined in grey wool. "I can sew. Nobody's ever asked me to." "Walk with me on Sunday." She buttons it on him. "Give me a little time." He goes to his shift believing the timing is theirs to choose.

### 5. Food

The covered market at Leadenhall, near closing. Alice is two stalls behind Nathaniel while he haggles over lathe tips. Behind her somebody tugs her sleeve. "Hey, what are you doing here?" The girl has slipped Ren the way a child slips a sitter. She holds up her hand. "Food." Alice buys her a bag of chestnuts and tells her to stay low behind the fruit stall. The girl stays low. It's the first time she's done what Alice asked.

### 6. The cold breaks out

Four of Lambrick's men have been working the aisles with a description of a woman from the Narrows. They find Alice under the paper stars and close in with clubs. Alice gets her knife out. There are four of them.

The girl stands up from behind the fruit stall.

The cold comes out of her all at once. Frost runs across the flags in a ring. Spears of ice go up from it, and three men go up with them, nine feet in the air against the iron pillars. A woman buying holly is caught at the edge. A boy with a basket. Then silence, and then the screaming. The girl's nose is bleeding. Alice takes her hand and pulls her down behind the stall.

### 7. Closing time at the covered market

Before the killing, Alice ducks a paper star and walks straight into the next one. Up in the roof a boy laughs. He tends the star lamps and takes a penny from each trader to lower the whole canopy at closing.

After the killing Nixon finds two hundred people pushing at shut gates. He opens both. His Lanterns leap to the iron galleries and light the aisles from above. The crush eases. Ajax looks up from the sisters’ trail and finds Nixon looking straight at him across the glass roof.

Alice shows the lamp boy a penny. He lowers the stars. From the gallery the lamps fill the frame. From underneath we can see two women walking slowly enough to keep the paper still. They gain one street. At the workshop door it is Nathaniel’s turn to decide.


### 8. One finger

The Lost One cannot uncurl her hand. Nathaniel offers his prototype to Alice, who asks her sister before touching her. One finger first. The crystal takes the cold. In Episode 7 both women will recognize the device when Nathaniel reaches for it. 


### 9. The waistcoat

Later. The girl is asleep on the blankets with her hand uncurled. Nathaniel is sweeping frost off the bench into a bucket. Alice hasn't sat down since she came through the door; she's standing where she can see both the window and him. Grise's envelope with his name on it is in her coat.

"You buttoned your waistcoat three holes crooked, your lordship. Is that the fashion in Mayfair, or does your valet dress you with his eyes shut?"

"I'm not a lord. I don't have a valet." He looks down at the waistcoat. "And it's an offset cut. For tool work. Not that anyone in the Reach has ever seen an engineer at work."

"I've seen plenty of men who think they're clever drop heavy iron on their own toes. You look the type."

He drops the dustpan. It misses his toes by an inch. Neither of them says anything. Then Alice turns to the window so he won't see her mouth, and for a second the only sound in the room is the girl breathing.

He re-does the waistcoat buttons when he thinks she isn't looking. She is.

### 10. The chair

The only chair in the workshop that doesn't squeak is missing a leg. Alice tries it, drops neatly into her sister's lap and, after a beat, announces that it takes two to sit in this house. The Lost One laughs before Alice does. They spend a minute trying out the other furniture with elaborate caution. It's their first joke since the river.

### 11. The Frost Killer

Guard headquarters, night. Nixon reports the market dead. Eldgard is already writing the statement on the family's paper: *The Frost Killer*, and a reward under it. "We don't know it's the same—" "The frost in my son's study. The frost at the market. Londoners need one name for what they're afraid of, Captain, or they'll find their own. Sign it as the officer in charge." Nixon signs. It's the first time his name goes under something he doesn't know is true.

### 12. The street talks

The Shadwell laundry after dark. A girl back from the market tells the tubs what she saw: Miriam Bounds, alive, killing men with ice. Grise stops ironing. Alice sat across from her this morning and asked whether there was any word. Grise sets the iron back on the stove very gently.

### 13. The wrong street

The Guard post, late. Nathaniel brings two mugs of chicory. Nixon's new keys are on the desk beside the search map, the Harding ring with the study key on it.

Nathaniel nudges them with a finger. "He gave you the study, Nix. Did you take the inventory?"

"He's in mourning, Nat."

"He's never in mourning. He's delegating. He gave you the keys because you do what you're told, and I ask where the lock was made."

Nixon doesn't look up from the map. "I do what I'm told because if I don't, I sleep in the mews. You get to be an angry philosopher because your name's carved on the front gate." He says it mildly, the way you'd mention the weather. It's the truest thing he's ever said to Nathaniel, and he moves straight on. "Where would you put them?"

Nathaniel reads the map like an engineer. The sisters are three doors from his workshop in Clerkenwell. He runs his pencil along the old storm drains, downhill, toward Gin Lane. "If she moves with the damp, she'll follow the drains. That's where I'd look." Twenty men move to Gin Lane. Clerkenwell stays open. "I don't know what I'd do without you tonight." "Neither do I."

### 14. The widows

First light in Gin Lane, snow on the frost. Hector stands beside a strongbox with Ajax and pays every family the ice touched, not only his own men's. The Hardings send no one. Tamara holds the ledger. "You're paying for the Hardings' monster." "I'm paying for the street's memory. In a month they'll need coal. They'll remember who came." "And if they ask why you came?" "Grief, my dear. It's the cheapest thing I'll ever buy." Tamara writes down every name. Two of the dead were Ajax's men. He watches her spell their names right, and ask their widows how. Hector never asked their names.

### 15. The names

Lambrick House, the morning room. Lord Lambrick sits by the fire in his wheeled chair, a blanket over his knees, the rattle in his chest going like a cart on cobbles. Hector, still in his snowy coat, tells him about Gin Lane: the strongbox, every family, the Hardings sending no one.

"Whose idea?"

"Mine."

The old man nods. Then he turns his head. "Read me the names, Tamara. My eyes are no good for ledgers."

Tamara doesn't open the ledger. She says them from memory, every one, with the street and the number of children. When she reaches Ajax's two men she gives their widows' names too.

Lord Lambrick listens with his eyes shut. "You paid them," he says to Hector. "She remembered them. Which do you think they'll remember?"

Hector laughs and kisses the top of his father's head. "Both of us, Father. That's why there are two of us." He goes to change his coat. Tamara stays by the fire. Her father doesn't say anything else to her. He doesn't have to; she heard it. So did Hector.

### 16. I know you (tag)

The workshop at dawn. Alice is asleep on the blankets by the bench. Nathaniel comes in from the Guard post and sits down at his drawing table without taking his coat off. The Lost One crosses the floor without a sound and stops beside him. She studies his face: the jaw, the ink on his cuffs, the eyes.

"I know you."
