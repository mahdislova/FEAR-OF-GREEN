# Episode 6 — The Remembering (aftermath of the Nixon break)

**Cold open:** None. Straight in.

**In short:** Nathaniel remembers 1873 and learns from Byron that the light is a woman in a pod. Alice confesses the contract. Eldgard cuts the East and Gin Lane explodes.

**Change:** Nathaniel learns what his father did to him.

**Foreground:** Nathaniel remembers 1873; Byron tells him what the light is.

**Background:** Nathaniel and Alice, and her contract confession; Nixon follows Nathaniel; Josephine and Grise; Grise tells Alice about the stepfather; Jennie and the Lost One; the Duke's ultimatum; Tamara gets the book; the East cut.

**Not in this episode:** Ajax, Marsh.

Among what comes back: the knowledge that the pod's machinery holds a signal down. **The trigger:** the two drawings laid edge to edge, and the gauntlet against the Lost One's wrist to read her cold. ## Scenes

### 1. The Works meeting — morning after the toast

Hector has left the engineer’s figures at Tamara’s place. She reads them while Eldgard asks how many hours cutting the East will buy. The engineer gives him the number. Tamara has a finger beside the same figure on the page. “And when do they come back on?” The engineer waits for Eldgard. Eldgard asks for the next estimate. The engineer turns his page.

Last night she wanted to hear what went missing from the papers. By the time the meeting ends she understands that nobody has agreed when the East will get its light back. She keeps her copy of the figures. Eldgard makes the decision at the end of the episode.


### 2. The corridor

Eldgard catches Tamara outside the meeting room. "You were right about the branch." "Then why—" "Because being right about one street is easy. You'll learn." He takes a book from under his arm. Alistair's, in his private cipher; no Harding has read it in a hundred years. The mother is failing faster than the yield admits, the child is loose, and he needs the old instructions. "Can you?" She takes the book. She doesn't ask what the instructions are for.

### 3. The tail

Harding House, breakfast. Nathaniel has slept here since he gave his second statement. Eldgard is warm with him, warmer than he has ever been. Nixon stands at the door in his Guard coat. When Nathaniel goes out, Nixon goes out too, twenty yards behind. Neither of them mentions it.

### 4. The Duke

The Works, Eldgard's office. The Duke arrives without an appointment and doesn't sit. The Lords are drafting a requisition. If the light fails in the frost, the Crown will send the Royal Engineers into the vaults with picks to find out why. He gives Eldgard until the frost breaks. "You asked me to be wrong in public. I won't be wrong twice." He goes out into the rain. Eldgard turns the engineer's figures face down.

### 5. Crumpets

The workshop. Alice brings Jennie, who has two squashed crumpets in her apron and an opinion about whose knitting teacher at the guild was worse. The Lost One watches them argue as if it's a game she's learning the rules of. Jennie toasts the crumpets on the cold-sink, which nobody thought of before. For a few minutes three young women laugh in a cold room.

### 6. Is she in there?

Alice goes down to check the alley door. Jennie is left alone with the Lost One. She sits beside her, looking at the line of Miriam's jaw. "Mim? It's Jen. You don't have to be anything. Just tell me it's you."

The girl turns her head. "You sang at the mangles when they squeaked. She liked that. It made her laugh."

*Liked.* Jennie hears it. She nods, as if that was the answer she came for. When Alice comes back up, Jennie is at the window with her back to the room, wiping her hands on her apron.

### 7. Josephine and Grise

The Shadwell laundry, the back room. Josephine has come in person. Grise tells her what she found in her own test: the girl living with Alice isn't Miriam. Josephine works out the timing herself: the night the ornament broke, the night of the bridge. The sister she hired is living with the face of the girl she killed.

"You were the one holding the knife. And you hired her sister."

There's a witness to the bridge. Josephine puts a purse on the ironing board. Grise makes it disappear.

### 8. Bring him back

The workshop, evening. The Lost One gives Alice both drawings, the outside of the ornament and the inside she drew from memory. "Bring him back. When it's safe." Alice folds them into her coat.

### 9. The ledger room

Night. Alice goes into the Shadwell laundry the Vykra way: across the drying lines, round the bell wire, through the shutter. Grise's ledger is open on the desk. The bridge night is there in Grise's private marks, and Alice can't read a word of it.

The lamp comes up. Grise is in the doorway. A heartbeat later Alice is behind her with a knife under her chin. Nobody saw her cross the room.

Grise doesn't struggle. She chooses cruelty. "Want to know what your sister paid to get in? Your stepfather. She was sixteen. The river." Alice's knife hand doesn't move. Then it does, away. Grise has won and they both know it. Alice goes out the way she came.

### 10. Left at the chimneys

Nathaniel goes over the Harding roofs the way he and Jamie did as boys. In the street below Nixon doesn't run. He walks. "You always went left at the chimneys." He follows.

### 11. Edge to edge

The workshop. Alice gives Nathaniel the drawings. He lays them edge to edge on the bench: the outside and the inside of the same wall. The lines meet. She remembers being inside it.

He asks the Lost One if he may put the gauntlet to her wrist to read her cold. She holds out her arm. The coil touches her skin. Her cold answers it, and green light jumps between them with a shriek like the ornament breaking. Nathaniel goes down.

### 12. 1873

Nathaniel at five, in bed. Something slips into the room. Eyes as limitless as the sky. Something goes into him and stays: the shape of a coil, the way a lock thinks. Under it all, a machine holding a signal down, the way you hold a hand over a mouth. Then his father in the doorway with a lantern, swinging it slowly, speaking softly. *A dream. You had a dream.* The light swings. The boy forgets.

He wakes on the workshop floor with blood in his mouth. Alice has his head in her lap. His genius was never his. His father didn't just forget him; he erased him.

### 13. Paid at first

Later. The Lost One is asleep behind the curtain. Nathaniel can't stop shaking. Alice tells him the truth because she can't let him lean on a lie. His mother hired her, through Grise, to get close to him and report where he went.

"Did she pay you to let me kiss you?"

"Paid at first. Never after."

He doesn't leave. Neither does she. That night they stop circling. Outside, in the snow, Nixon looks up at the lit window for a long time. Then he decides.

### 14. You were always enough

Harding House, dawn. Byron is laying the breakfast silver. Nathaniel stands in the pantry door with dried blood on his collar. "Byron. What's under the Works?"

Thirty years of silence give out. "A woman. In a pod. That's the light, Master Nat. That's all it ever was." And then, because it's the thing he's wanted to say for sixteen years: "Your father didn't make you clever. You were always enough."

### 15. Two books

Night, cross-cut. In the Harding library Tamara has Alistair's cipher open under the lamp. The first line breaks. Ada brings tea, sees which book is open, sets the cup down very carefully, and goes. Across the city a runner brings Hector word from the Works: the East End is to be cut tonight. Hector looks at the drawer where he locked Grise's audit of the Gin Lane housings. He doesn't open it. He goes back to his dinner. Two books, two families, and each one shut on what it knows.

### 16. Gin Lane washhouse

The engineer has pencilled “fractured district housing” beside Gin Lane. Tamara asks if they can empty the branch before the cut. Yes, if Eldgard gives up another hour of West End supply. He keeps the West lit: the hospitals, the pumps and the Works are there, and the engineer's figures say the East's old housings will fail first either way. It's the defensible choice, and it's still the one that kills Gin Lane. Tamara sends word to clear the washhouse. Her runner reaches the near end of the street.

At the washhouse Byron helps a laundress named Nell lower the drying racks to make room for families coming in out of the cold. An old man has brought his rent tin. Nell makes Byron take it for safekeeping so the man will lie down. When the lamps die, Nell is halfway across the floor with a blanket. Green light comes back through the soles of her shoes.

The shot stays outside long enough to see light run the length of the block. The street lifts. Both rows of houses go with it. In the sudden quiet the rent tin rolls across the washhouse tiles and comes to rest against something green. A tight shell has crystallized around Byron. His eyes are open inside it. There is nowhere left for the runner to deliver the warning.
