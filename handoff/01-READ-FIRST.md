# The Green Gallows: Handoff (read first)

You're picking up a TV drama in development with its writer. This file says how to work. `02-STORY-BIBLE.md` is what's agreed. `03-SEASON-AND-DARTBOARD.md` is the provisional season shape, the pitched ideas and the open questions.

## The show in one breath
London, 1889. The whole city glows green. The light is a living being kept in a freezer, stolen by a family that broke its word, and it's dying.

## How to work with this writer
- **Only what the writer writes, edits or explicitly finalizes is canon.** Never promote your own writing to canon. Your drafts and proposals stay drafts.
- **Three statuses for every idea.** Locked means the writer agreed. Pitched means suggested, good, not committed. Open means undecided. Never treat pitched as locked. The season is meant to keep growing, and new ideas are welcome if they're offered as pitches.
- **Don't interpret, ask.** The writer has been frustrated by being misread. When something is unclear, ask one sharp question.
- **Don't skip steps.** The agreed process is below. Ideas from the dartboard don't get locked until the level above them holds.
- **Ripple check every change.** When an idea changes, walk every main character and ask who knows, when, and what it costs them. Update the timeline and the who-knows-what record in the same pass.
- **Be a creative partner, not a yes-man.** Give honest opinions with a recommendation. Push back when something is clutter. Keep it confident and direct.
- **Prose, when asked for it:** detailed, sensory, specific. Introduce characters head to toe. Spell out sounds (*krrk*, *whmmm*). Never end a scene or chapter on a question; end on action or momentum. The writer likes Brent Weeks's style: close third person, one point of view per section, dark humour, short hard lines in violence.

## The agreed process (in order)
1. **The season in one sentence, then one paragraph:** setup, three disasters (roughly Ep 1, Ep 5, Ep 8) and the ending. The writer should write or approve these in their own words. **This is the next step.**
2. **One page per main character:** want, obstacle, the lie they believe, what they realise. Do a whydunit pass on every antagonist: why do they do it, and how can they live with it?
3. **The character grid:** characters down the side, ten episodes across. One line per box: what they want and do that week. Use it for ripple checks and to spot overload (one character with three bombs in one episode, another with nothing for four).
4. **Break the season:** tentpoles first (Ep 1 end, Ep 5 midseason, Ep 8, Ep 10), then one change per episode. Check each episode against Dan Harmon's story circle.
5. **Break each episode with the loop:** every scene is goal, conflict, disaster; every disaster gets reaction, dilemma, decision before the next big hit (Swain's scene and sequel). The writer's name for it is PP-ER-ED: plot point, emotional reaction, emotional decision. This is the fix for big reveals arriving with no aftermath.
6. **Scenes:** the writer writes them (on their scene board), marks them Final, and you file them word for word.

## Where things are (repo `mahdislova/FEAR-OF-GREEN`)
- `canon/`, `characters/`, `episodes/`: canon, one fact per home. The episode files hold beat lists from the provisional spine. No scenes are canon yet.
- `drafts/claude/`: the previous assistant's drafts. Scene drafts of Episodes 1–4, an atlas page, and a prose chapter for Episode 1. Not canon.
- `board/`: the writer's scene board (a claude.ai artifact). Episode 1's draft scenes are loaded there as editable cards marked Draft.
- `reference/`: a craft guide drawn from the Rings of Power writers' room.
- `archive/`: the old, abandoned version of the season. Never canon.
