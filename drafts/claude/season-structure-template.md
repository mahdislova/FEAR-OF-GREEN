# Season structure template and story-breaking toolkit

**Claude's proposal. Not canon.** Part 1 is general craft, Part 2 maps our ten episodes onto it, Part 3 is the order of work.

## Part 1: the toolkit

### The season is one long film
Treat the ten episodes as one feature cut into ten pieces. The big structural beats land at roughly fixed points:

| Beat | Where (of 10) | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening image / inciting incident | Ep 1 | The world as it was, then the thing that breaks it |
| Lock-in (break into two) | End of Ep 2 – Ep 3 | The lead makes a choice they can't take back |
| Pinch point 1 | Ep 4 | The antagonist force shows its full power; the stakes become clear |
| Midpoint | Ep 5 | A false victory or false defeat; the lead goes from reacting to acting; the stakes double |
| Pinch point 2 | Ep 6–7 | The antagonist hits again, harder; what's been built starts to break |
| All is lost | End of Ep 7 – Ep 8 | The worst outcome happens; "a whiff of death" (someone or something dies) |
| Dark night | Ep 8 – top of Ep 9 | The lead sits with the loss and makes the choice that turns them |
| Break into three | Ep 9 | A new plan, built from everything learned |
| The penultimate set piece | Ep 9 | The biggest action episode; it must end in a reversal, not a win |
| Climax and resolution | Ep 10 | The final choice that answers the season's question; the closing image mirrors the opening one |

Each lead gets their own version of this arc, and the leads' darkest moments should be **staggered** so the back half never goes slack.

### Breaking an episode
1. **Know the change.** One sentence: what's different by the end. (We have this.)
2. **Foreground and background.** The A story gets about 50–60% of the screen time, the B story about 25–30%, a C story or runner the rest.
3. **Cards.** One card per scene, colour-coded by storyline. Write it loose enough to move around. Lay out the A story alone first, then the B, then blend them.
4. **Act-outs.** An hour of drama runs four or five acts. Every act ends on a reversal, a threat or a reveal, never on a resolution.
5. **Know the tag first.** Break toward the ending; if you don't know the last image, you can't aim the scenes.

### Testing every scene
- **The turn.** Every scene enters with one charge and leaves with the opposite: hope to fear, safe to exposed. If nothing turns, cut it or merge it into another scene. (McKee)
- **"But" and "therefore", never "and then".** Each scene should cause the next one or complicate it. (Trey Parker and Matt Stone)
- **Want, obstacle, tactic, outcome.** Who wants what in the scene, what stops them, what they try, how it ends. If nobody wants anything, it's not a scene.
- **The logistics pass.** Where is everyone, how did they get there, what do they know right now. (This is our CLAUDE.md logic check, applied scene by scene.)
- **Enter late, leave early.** Start a scene after it has begun and leave before it ends.

### Tricks
- **Break from the end backwards** when the middle won't come. Ask what the climax needs to be true, then plant it.
- **The circle inside each episode** (Dan Harmon): a want, unfamiliar ground, a cost paid, a change. Every episode should run a full circle even while the season arc stays open.
- **Runners.** A small recurring thread (Ada's lamps, the chair, the coat) gives an episode texture without costing it structure.
- **Pay the character before the plot.** If a twist doesn't change somebody, it's clutter.

## Part 2: our season on the template

| Beat | Our episode | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inciting incident | Ep 1: the ornament breaks; Jamie and Miriam die | Strong |
| Lock-in | Ep 2 end / Ep 3: Alice brings the girl home; Nathaniel hides the sisters and lies to Nixon | Strong |
| Pinch point 1 | Ep 4: the hearing; "when the mother dies, use the child" | Strong |
| Midpoint | Ep 5: the dinner; Nixon hands Eldgard the journal; "Find her" | Good. The hunt flips: Nathaniel is now the hunted |
| Pinch point 2 | Ep 6 tag: Gin Lane explodes | Strong |
| All is lost | Ep 7–8: Miriam is dead; the Lost One is taken to the pod | Good, but Ep 7 is overloaded |
| Dark night | Ep 9 opening: Ada's death | Good, for Nixon |
| Break into three | Ep 9: Marsh and the way in | Thin. The plan doesn't exist on the page yet |
| Penultimate set piece | Ep 9: storming the Works | **Hole.** One line long, and the tag is open |
| Climax | Ep 10: the pod; the light ends; she stays | Strong as an ending, but it's a list, not a sequence |

**The staggered darkest moments already work:** Alice in Ep 7 (Miriam is dead), Nathaniel in Ep 8 (the relapse: he hands the Lost One over and says "I will"), Nixon in Ep 9 (Ada). This is the best structural asset of the back half. Protect it.

### What the template exposes
1. **Ep 9 has no reversal.** If the storming succeeds, Ep 10 is a victory lap. The penultimate episode should end with the plan failing: they reach Eldgard and lose, or they win the room and lose the Lost One to the pod. That's what forces the Ep 10 choice. **Your decision:** how the storm fails.
2. **Ep 7 is three episodes' worth.** The raid, Gin Lane's dead, the identity reveal, the Jamie confession, the pod, the coal deal, the separation and the nosebleed. Something has to move: the raid to the end of Ep 6, or the pod to Ep 8.
3. **Threads that vanish.** Ajax isn't in anything after Ep 5. Grise is gone after Ep 7, Jennie after Ep 8, Byron for Eps 8–10. An enforcer who disappears for half the season is a loaded gun nobody fires. Ep 10's dark London on Lambrick coal is the natural place to fire it. Byron's one turn, if you keep it, has to happen in Ep 9 or 10, and he isn't in either.
4. **Parked logistics.** Ep 6 ("how they arrange a safe return"), Ep 8 (the arrest site and how Josephine finds the Lost One), Ep 9 (the way into the Works). These are the questions breaking the scenes will force.

## Part 3: the order of work

1. **The big-beat pass first, before any scenes.** Settle the four things above, in this order: how Ep 9 fails; what moves out of Ep 7; what happens to Ajax (and Grise and Jennie); the parked logistics. These are decisions about the spine, and every scene below them depends on them.
2. **File Ep 1 from the board**, exactly as written.
3. **Break Eps 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 in order**, to the Ep 2 standard. Each break goes to `drafts/claude/` and becomes canon only when you finalize it on the board.
