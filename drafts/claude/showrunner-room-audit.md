# Showrunner Room Audit: Season One

Run on the season as it stands on main (commit `0ba512b`), through the four-showrunner framework: Gilligan (causality), Mazin (theme and grounded systems), Armstrong (subtext and psychology) and Gilroy (logistics and minor agendas). **This is diagnosis and pitches. Nothing here changes canon until the writer approves it.**

It doesn't repeat the Weeks audit. Where it overlaps, it says so.

---

## 1. Beginning → middle → end: the spine

**The dramatic question, from the text:** will Nathaniel decide that London's light isn't worth a child in a freezer, and pay for that decision himself?

**The theme, from the text:** is keeping someone the same as loving them?

**The beginning (Eps 1–2): the lie and the false equilibrium.** Nathaniel's lie is that being useful is the same as being loved. The pressure that makes it unsustainable: the empty chair, the misfire, his brother dead in the study. The rules are planted: the light has a source, the source is dying, Harding technology contains cold. **This holds.**

**The middle (Eps 5–6): the point of no return.** Ep 5 has Nixon discover the hiding, and Eldgard read "Find her". Ep 6 has Nathaniel learn his father erased him and that the light is a woman. **Belief proven false: yes, his usefulness was planted, and the father he was trying to be useful to did it.** This is a strong double midpoint.

**The end (Eps 8–10): the verdict.** He opens the pod with the planted knowledge, a capability from the beginning, not a miracle. Lambrick wins; the city hates him. **The ending answers the question at a cost.** Good.

**Where the spine drifts:**
1. **Eps 8–10 lose the "hunt for the killer" engine and don't replace it cleanly.** The investigation ends in Ep 7 (he learns she did it). In Ep 8 he confirms her confession to Nixon, then rescues her the same day. The spine's turn from *justice* to *mercy* happens in one episode with one trigger: he learns about the pod, from an unnamed source (see section 2). It needs its own clearly caused step.
2. **The ship isn't on the spine.** It's the final image of the season and is caused by nothing in it (section 5, fix 1).
3. **The Newgate set piece (Ep 9) is tonally off-spine.** A mountain prison with dropping towers and patrol wings is spectacle from a different show. See section 2.

**Subplots, and whether they press on the theme:**

| Subplot | Presses on "is keeping love?" | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alice and the Lost One | Holding on vs letting go | Core |
| Nathaniel and Nixon | The kept son vs the spare | Core |
| Josephine's penance | Keeping your guilt close | Core |
| Tamara, Nixon and the engagement | A daughter kept as currency | Core |
| Hector and coal | Owning vs keeping | Core |
| The Hargraves (Ada, Marsh) | A family kept as witnesses | Core |
| The Duke | A friendship spent for the light | Good, contained |
| Byron's ring and shell | Not yet tied to the theme | **Detached.** It's a device, not an argument. See section 2. |

## 2. Causality and "nonsense" (Gilligan and Gilroy)

**Gilligan's rule:** coincidence may get a character into trouble, never out of it. **Gilroy's:** follow the logistics; nobody is a prop.

| # | Where | The problem | Type | Proposed fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Ep 4, Grise's ultimatum** | Grise threatens to tell Nathaniel about Alice's contract. **But the contract's client is Josephine, his mother.** Exposing it burns Grise's own paying client, and Alice knows who the client is. The threat isn't credible, and Alice would see that. *This was my fix, and it fails the room's test.* | Idiot plot | Grise threatens what she really would do: **sell the girl to Lambrick's man.** "Bring her in, or I give Ajax your sister's address." Alice brings her in to buy time and control the meeting. Grise sells a sighting anyway: the betrayal Alice should have expected from a woman who sells to everyone. |
| 2 | **Eps 5–7, "Find her" goes nowhere** | Eldgard gives the order at the end of Ep 5. Eps 6 and 7 show no hunt. In Ep 8 Josephine has to supply the lead. | Consequence amnesia | Make it bite. | 3 | **Ep 7, the riot** | The Lost One saves Jennie in the riot, but she's supposed to be hidden in the workshop. No reason is given for her to be in the street. | Teleporting motivation | Fixed by #2: she's being moved because the hunt is closing. |

## 3. The thematic argument (Mazin)

**Is the theme tested by consequences or just talked about?** Tested, mostly. Gin Lane, the frozen East, the pod and the buttons are all consequences.

**Each character holds a distinct stance on "is keeping love?":**

| Character | Stance |
| --- | --- |
| Eldgard | Keeping is duty: the many outweigh the few |
| Josephine | Keeping is penance: control is protection |
| Alice | Holding on is love |
| Nathaniel | Wants to be kept; builds cages without knowing it |
| Nixon | Was kept, and called it belonging |
| Hector | Keeping means owning |
| Grise | Keeping means selling |
| Tamara | Kept as currency, then sets herself free |
| Ada | Keeps the faith because the alternative is unbearable |
| The Lost One | Stays because she's free to leave: the answer |

**The weakest point: Eldgard's counter-argument isn't strong enough.** Mazin's rule is that the wrong choice must look rational from the inside. Right now, cutting the East to keep the West lit reads as class cruelty, and the Gin Lane blast makes him a villain. **The season needs at least one scene where Eldgard is right.**

- **Fix:** make the Ep 6 cut an **engineering triage**, not a class choice. The West holds the hospitals, the water pumps and the Works itself; the East's housings are the oldest and the first to fail. The engineer's figures prove it. Tamara's question ("when do they come back on?") still goes unanswered. Gin Lane then becomes **the price of a defensible decision**, which is much harder to watch.
- **Ep 9's confession** should put the full argument in his mouth, with numbers: how many die in a winter without the light. And **Ep 10 has to prove him partly right:** people freeze because Nathaniel opened the pod, on screen. The audience should finish the season unsure Nathaniel was right. That's the cost the framework asks for.

## 4. Subtext and psychology (Armstrong)

**Dialogue that says the feeling out loud.** Several lines (many of them mine) are epigrams rather than deflections. Armstrong's people hide behind status and small talk.

| Line | Problem | Alternative direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hector: "Grief, my dear. It's the cheapest thing I'll ever buy." | Too self-aware. He narrates his own strategy. | He doesn't explain. Tamara asks why; he says "Count the coins, Tamara." She works it out herself, and so does the audience. |
| The Duke: "You're asking me to be wrong in public, so you don't have to be." | Lands, but declarative. | Keep it, but cut "For Jamie. Not for the light." The silence before his "yes" says it. |
| Josephine: "I chose you so I'd have to look at you." | A confession, and her rule is that she never confesses. | She deflects even now: "You button better than my maid ever did." Alice understands, and Josephine has still said nothing. |
| Eldgard: "Did you love me, or did you keep me?" / "Yes." | It's a signature line. Keep it. | It works because it's a deflection that tells the truth. |

**Emotional shifts missing their micro-steps:**
1. **Nathaniel, Ep 7 to Ep 8:** he tries to restrain her, then confirms her confession to Nixon, then rescues her, all in a day. The missing step is the regression Armstrong would insist on: **confirming the confession to Nixon is Nathaniel being the useful son one more time**, handing his father justice. Play it as that, a relapse, so the rescue is him breaking it for good.
2. **Alice, Ep 7 night to Ep 8 morning:** she sends the Lost One away, then returns by choice. What changes overnight? Give her one concrete thing. Jennie's message that the girl is bleeding from the nose (the new rule) would work: the cost of the cold is now the cost of her saving Jennie, and Alice can't hold the grudge against that.
3. **Nixon, Ep 8 to Ep 9:** the Hargrave pages, then Ada's death, then the break-out. The steps exist. Protect them.

**Regression to protect:** Eldgard breaks over the journal, then the quiet of the house returns. That's Armstrong's circular loop exactly: the wound doesn't heal, it closes over.

## 5. The fix list: five highest-impact changes

1. **Give the ship a cause: opening the pod calls it.** Plant it in the Ep 1 cold open: Alistair silences something on the wreck, a beacon that has been held quiet in the pod's machinery ever since. In Ep 6, Nathaniel's restored memory includes the knowledge that the pod holds that signal down. In Ep 10 he opens the pod knowing what it might call, or not knowing; it's the writer's choice. Either way **the ship stops being a miracle and becomes the consequence of his choice,** and Season 2's threat is something Nathaniel did. Morgana stays helpless and in the background; the machine, not she, sends the signal.
2. **Make "Find her" bite.** Eldgard's hunt closes on the workshop through Eps 6–7. It moves the Lost One into the streets for the riot, and gives Alice's sending her away a second reason.
3. **Fix Grise's Ep 4 threat.** "Bring her in, or I give Ajax her address." It's credible, true to Grise, and pays off when she sells the sighting anyway.
4. **Make Eldgard right once.** The East cut is engineering triage, the hospitals and pumps are in the West, and Gin Lane is the price of a defensible choice. In Ep 10 the dark kills on screen.
5. **Close the logistics holes:** Josephine's alibi in Ep 1 (Byron covers for her), the source of Nathaniel's pod knowledge in Ep 8 (Nixon), the ring's logic in Ep 6 (Jamie's ring, or a tool for the core's guard), and a decision on Newgate's geography.

## Questions for the writer
1. The ship as the consequence of opening the pod: yes or no? And does Nathaniel know what it might call?
2. "Find her" closing on the workshop through Eps 6–7: yes?
3. Grise's threat becomes Ajax and the address: yes?
4. The East cut as engineering triage: yes?
5. The logistics fixes: Byron covers Josephine; Nixon tells Nathaniel about the pod; Jamie's ring or a tool for the core's guard; and Newgate: keep the mountain or ground it?
