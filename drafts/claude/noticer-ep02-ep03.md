# The Noticer: Episodes 2 and 3

The Thematic, Fun and Exciting Noticer, run on Episodes 2 and 3 as they stand on main. **Outcome:** the writer kept two pitches, Miriam's throw planted in Ep 1 and Tamara almost telling Nixon in Ep 3, and both are now filed in canon. The rest were not adopted.

**Every suggestion is a pitch.** Nothing changes in canon without the writer's approval.

### The most alive moment
**Scene 9: Alice sits down on the wet flags and waits until the girl takes her sleeve.** Alice doesn't grab or hold on. She waits to be chosen. It's the season's question (is keeping someone the same as loving them?) in a single gesture, an episode in, and it's the opposite of who Alice is.

### The longest stretch with the same feeling
**Scenes 10–13: tea, the Bounds room, Nixon and Tamara, Ren.** Four warm, tender scenes in a row. Each is good on its own, but together they let the dread from Grise (scene 2) and the cold skin (scene 11) cool off before the parents and the gloves.

### Four pitches

**1. Plant Miriam's one-handed throw in Episode 1.**
- *Where:* the Narrows takedown (Ep 1), paid off in Ep 2 scenes 7 and 9.
- *What the audience gets now:* in scene 9 the boy says "she threw me one-handed" and Alice is relieved, but the audience has never seen Miriam do it, so they're told the recognition rather than feeling it.
- *Shootable action:* in the Narrows fight, Miriam drops a man one-handed with that same throw, the move Alice has watched her whole life.
- *What it changes later:* in scene 7 the audience recognises the throw a beat before the girl does, and in scene 9 Alice's relief becomes theirs too. It also sharpens the dread, because Miriam's body still knows how to fight.
- *Logic check:* the Narrows raid already has Miriam fighting. Nothing new is needed.

**2. Ren's fingers stay on the wrist too long.**
- *Where:* Ep 2 scene 13, Ren's rooms. This breaks the warm run.
- *What the audience gets now:* a kind healer with a reassuring explanation.
- *Shootable action:* Ren takes the girl's pulse as any healer would. Her fingers stay a beat too long. She counts under her breath, frowns, and lets go. Then she gives Alice the comforting explanation anyway, and takes the coin.
- *What it changes later:* Ren becomes the first person who knows the body is wrong and chooses kindness over truth. That's a small lie that mirrors Alice's own. When Ep 7 reveals the truth, Ren's face here re-reads.
- *Logic check:* a healer checking a pulse is natural. It fits the rule that the cold holds Miriam's body as it was in the river.

**3. The last button.**
- *Where:* Ep 2 scene 11, before Alice takes her to Ren.
- *What the audience gets now:* Alice dressing her, tender and practical.
- *Shootable action:* Alice fastens the girl's coat out of habit, one button after another. At the top button, the girl stops Alice's hand and does it herself, correctly. It's what the child taught her in scene 5.
- *What it changes later:* it starts a motif that's already in canon. Alice fastens Josephine's cuff buttons in Ep 4 and stops at two in Ep 8. Now the buttons mean something from the start: Alice's love is doing things *for* people, and the first person who loves her back insists on doing one thing herself. It argues the season's question without a word of dialogue.
- *Logic check:* she learned the button in scene 5 and wants Alice's approval. It's consistent.

**4. Eldgard cuts off the answer himself.**
- *Where:* Ep 2 scene 14, the study.
- *What the audience gets now:* Eldgard asking where Jamie went next.
- *Shootable action:* Nathaniel starts to say it: "When I got there, he was still—", and Eldgard talks over him: "Where did he go after the roof?" Nathaniel stops. He never finishes the sentence.
- *What it changes later:* this sets up the **Ep 5 tag**. When Eldgard reads in Nixon's journal that Jamie was alive when Nathaniel reached him, the audience remembers that he was told and talked over it. His grief there becomes self-inflicted. It's the devastation the writer asked for, planted three episodes earlier.
- *Logic check:* it matches canon. Eldgard's first question that night was whether Jamie opened anything, and he's controlling the narrative.

### The beat to protect in the edit
**Alice on the wet flags, waiting for the girl to take her sleeve (scene 9).** Don't cut it short or score over it. Hold until the girl moves.

## Episode 3: The Frost Killer

### The most alive moment
**The paper stars coming down, and two women walking slowly enough to keep the paper still.** Beauty and dread at once: from above, nothing but lamps; from below, the most hunted woman in London walking under them at a funeral pace.

### The longest stretch with the same feeling
**The opening run of secrecy: Jennie's warning, the laundry, and Grise's lie.** Three scenes of people hiding things from each other before anything breaks. It works, but the stretch needs the market's violence to arrive on time.

### Four pitches

**1. The first nosebleed (the new rule's first appearance).**
- *Where:* straight after the ice outburst.
- *What the audience gets now:* bodies in the air, and the sisters running.
- *Shootable action:* as they run, the girl's nose starts to bleed. Alice wipes it with her thumb without breaking stride, and the blood smears across the Vykra knot inside her glove. The bystanders the ice killed are behind them. Alice sees them, and keeps walking.
- *What it changes later:* it's the first time the audience sees the cold cost something. Alice's choice to keep walking past the dead to protect her sister is holding on, her flaw, shown in one ugly action (the audit flagged that her reaction was missing). And the blood on the knot ties the cost of her sister's power to the guild that owns her.
- *Logic check:* the rule is now canon. Alice is a glim-runner who's seen blood. Under threat, protecting her sister comes first.

**2. Grise re-addresses the envelope.**
- *Where:* when word reaches Grise from the street.
- *What the audience gets now:* Grise realising Alice is playing her.
- *Shootable action:* Grise takes Alice's contract envelope out of her ledger, the one with Nathaniel Harding's name on it, and sets it to one side, on top. She doesn't say a word.
- *What it changes later:* it plants Ep 4's ultimatum (bring your sister in, or Nathaniel learns who's paying you). The audience sees Grise pick her weapon an episode before she uses it. That's the antagonist moving first.
- *Logic check:* Grise holds the contract; Josephine paid through her.

**3. The crystal sings to her.**
- *Where:* "One finger," in the workshop, when the cold-sink takes her cold.
- *What the audience gets now:* relief, and Alice asking before touching.
- *Shootable action:* as the crystal takes the cold, the girl closes her eyes and leans her cheek against it, like a child against a parent's chest. Alice reads it as relief. Nathaniel is pleased his device works.
- *What it changes later:* in Ep 6 Nathaniel learns the light is a woman in a pod. Gallowglass is her blood, and he put her mother on the girl's hand. This image re-reads as the cruellest thing in the show, made by the kindest person in it. It's Weeks's retroactive recontextualisation, and it doesn't break the rule that Morgana stays in the background and helpless: the crystal does nothing; the child is the one who reaches for it.
- *Logic check:* the cold-sink uses a gallowglass core, as in canon. Nobody in the room knows what the crystal is.

**4. Tamara almost tells him.**
- *Where:* Nixon and Tamara's private meeting.
- *What the audience gets now:* love and a delay.
- *Shootable action:* she has the engagement letter in her glove. When he asks about walking her home openly, her fingers go to it, and stay there. She asks for time instead.
- *What it changes later:* the Ep 5 toast lands harder, because the audience watched her nearly tell him and choose not to.
- *Logic check:* in canon she already knows about the Harding arrangement. This only gives the knowing a physical object.

### The beat to protect in the edit
**"One finger first."** Alice asking her sister before she touches her. It's the season's question as an action, and the opposite of everything the Hardings do to the things they keep.
