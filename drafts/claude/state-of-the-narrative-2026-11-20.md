## Where the work has moved

Ep 2 is fully broken (16 scenes). Ep 5 is fully broken (14 scenes). Ep 6 has the Works meeting and Gin Lane. Ep 9 has the Newgate break-out through the draw room. Ep 3 has two scenes (the latest revision was reverted). Eps 1, 4, 7, 8 and 10 are still beat lists.

The improvements are real. In order of what they changed:

1. **Gin Lane is now a decision, not an accident.** The engineer's two sheets, the split housing on the press, Eldgard's "two estimates", Tamara's "and when do they come back on?", the extra hour of West End supply he won't give up, the runner who reaches the near end of the street. The light's cost has moved from mythic (a woman in a pod) to arithmetic (an hour of Mayfair against a washhouse full of families). That's the most important upgrade in the season.
2. **Tamara became a player.** She bargained for the toast, took the chair, read the figures, tried to clear the washhouse and failed. Her Ep 10 depots are now earned by Gin Lane instead of announced.
3. **Jennie got agency.** The signed statement in a public room, Ajax trapped by his own offer, Grise unable to punish her in front of the women. She's turning into the show's quiet spine.
4. **Eldgard got a face.** "I should have gone myself," then he asks again where Jamie went. One beat holds the whole man. The ribbon pin with Josephine does the same for her.
5. **The Hargrave chain is engineered.** Spectacles → Nixon must hand them over himself → the gaoler records the gift under his name → Marsh reads the district figures → the boyhood draw-room service → the cleaning wheel in Ep 9. Plus "you can cut it yourself this time" → Nixon holding the lamp on the stair.

## Stakes

**Working.** Three layers that now press on each other:
- **City:** the light is dying, and keeping the West lit kills the East. Gin Lane proves it on screen.
- **Moral:** a child in a freezer against London's warmth. The Ep 4 tag lets the audience carry it before anyone on screen does.
- **Personal:** Nathaniel shelters his brother's suspected killer; Alice spies on the man she's falling for for the woman who killed her sister; Nixon investigates his best friend for the father who keeps him.

**Not working yet.**
- **The clock is decorative.** "THE LIGHT HAS 49 DAYS" sits in `world-and-rules.md` as if locked, but the dartboard lists it as pitched, and no episode pins a day, a yield figure or a date to Morgana's death. Nothing tells us how many days the wedding happens before the end. **Pitch:** let the engineer's sheet be the clock. Every time the figure changes on paper it's a new card, so the countdown lives inside the paper engine (below) rather than as a title.
- **Morgana's stakes are abstract until Ep 8.** The glim "Weeping" (carried over, unreviewed) is the one device that would let London hear her every week. Either use it or cut it.
- **The frozen eastern villages** are still an open mystery with no payoff slot. A season that ends on "no new mythology after Ep 8" can't leave that hanging; decide in the next lore pass whether it's simply the failing light (then say so by Ep 4) or something stranger (then it belongs to Season 2 and should be flagged as such).

## The story engine

On paper the engine is: investigation (who killed Jamie) + concealment (Nathaniel hides the suspect from the friend running the inquiry) + dramatic irony (we know about Josephine and about the Lost One) + a dying light.

What the broken scenes have actually built is sharper than that. **The show runs on paper.** Nixon's journal, pen down then pen up. The first statement Nixon repeats to Hector, which Nathaniel lets stand. The second statement. Jennie's signature. The gaoler's register. The engineer's two sheets. Tamara's copy of the figures. The relief clerk asking Byron for names. Byron giving his own name as witness. Alistair's cipher. The Hargrave pages. "What gets written down" is already in the theme line. In practice it's the mechanism: lies get expensive the moment they're written, and truth becomes a weapon the moment someone signs it. Lean into it on purpose.

The second engine is **gesture call-backs**, and they all ask the same question, *who gets a seat*:
- **Chairs:** "That's Nixon's chair, Nat" → the three-legged chair, "it takes two to sit in this house" → the empty place on the bench → Hector drawing up a chair for Tamara → "it takes two to sit here as well" in Ep 8.
- **Buttons:** the children at the awning → Josephine's pearl cuff → Alice stopping after two.
- **The squeeze:** Jamie's receiving-line code → Nixon at the toast, Nathaniel covering his hand: *stay*.

That's theme (spare and heir, where you belong) done entirely in props.

**Where the engine stalls: the investigation resolves by confession, not detection.** Nathaniel's season mission is to find his brother's killer, but between Ep 3 and Ep 7 his detective work is the shard and a drawing. The answer arrives because the Lost One's memory happens to come back. The audience will feel the hunt go slack in Eps 4–6. **Pitch:** let Nathaniel's own evidence trigger the Ep 7 memory. He already has the shard, the exterior drawing, her interior drawing and the containment device both women will recognise. If he rebuilds the ornament's seal to test a theory, and the Lost One's memory resolves when she sees it, then his investigation causes the confession and his grief causes the restraint. Same beats, but he's driving them.

## Character dynamics

**Nathaniel / Nixon: the best thing in the show.** The toast squeeze is the most alive moment written so far: Nixon gives the gesture as if happy, Nathaniel answers *stay*, and Nixon has to stand there and be thanked. Protect it in the edit. Ep 5 is now smarter than the spine: Nathaniel volunteers the shard, so the break is half confession, half discovery. That makes it adult.
**The problem:** after the break Nixon disappears for two episodes. His Ep 6 and Ep 7 thread cells are empty or belong to Nathaniel, and he comes back as POV of Ep 8. Canon says "Ep 6 absorbs the break with Nixon", but nothing on the page does. **Pitch:** one Nixon beat in each of 6 and 7. In 6, Eldgard hands him the Frost Killer hunt formally, with Nathaniel's statement as the first exhibit. In 7, Nixon is the officer the Hardings send to Gin Lane to announce that the Frost Killer did it, and Byron gives his witness name to *him*. That puts him on record against the truth one episode before he takes her in.

**Nixon / Tamara / Nathaniel.** The triangle finally has teeth because Tamara chose the chair over Nixon in front of him. **Gap:** Nathaniel never learns about Nixon and Tamara. It's a loaded gun sitting in the continuity table with no shot fired. It belongs in Ep 9 or 10, ideally on the same side of the Works door, when Nathaniel can least afford it and is most able to forgive it.

**Alice / the Lost One: the heart, and the best-built.** Shift on the pillow → shift that scratches; chair joke → chair payoff; eight posts → nine. The design is right.
**Problem:** Ep 7 is carrying Gin Lane's aftermath, Byron's witness, a riot, Jennie's rescue, the stepfather confirmed, the identity reveal, the Jamie confession, the restraint, the escape, the coal deal and Jennie's shelter. That's two episodes. **Pitch:** move "Tell my father yes" out of Ep 7 and give it to Tamara at the start of Ep 8. She has the figures and the East's dead; she brokers it with Nathaniel herself, which also sets up the altar and Nixon's ring.

**Alice / Nathaniel: the thinnest-drawn of the leads' dynamics.** Ep 5 scene 11 (he gives her the true day; she puts his coat back on the peg) is lovely. Then: Ep 6 "the night they stop circling" (unbroken) → Ep 7 divided → Ep 8 she confesses the contract, which "compounds the damage" → Ep 9 they storm the Works side by side → Ep 10 "Us" at the lock. **There is no repair beat between Ep 8 and Ep 10.** "Us" won't land unless something happens in Ep 9 that is theirs alone.

**Josephine / Alice: the dread engine.** Correctly built as irony, not twist. The cuff is the spine. **Thin spot:** Josephine is barely on screen in Eps 3–5. One more scene where she is kind to Alice, and means it, would make Ep 8's two buttons hurt more.

**Eldgard / Nixon.** "Did you love me, or did you keep me?" "Yes." That's earned by the dossier. It's not yet earned on the page, because the scenes give Eldgard and Nixon only the keys (Ep 2) and the village report. **Pitch:** a small Ep 5 or 6 moment where Eldgard is genuinely fatherly to Nixon and it's also surveillance. He asks after Ada, say, and Nixon realises later that he was being asked where his loyalties sleep.

**Hector: the arch-nemesis is being steered.** In both broken scenes (Ep 2 coal, Ep 5 toast) his daughter talks him out of his first move. That's great for Tamara and dangerous for Hector, who wins the season. Worse, the moment he learns what the light is (the hinge of his "greed curve") isn't placed in any episode. **Pitch:** give him the one scene where nobody steers him: the engineer brings him the secret, and Hector's first question is how long she'd last if you drew harder.

**Byron.** His "secret" slot is open. Leave it empty. His lie is that loyalty means silence. He breaks silence twice, privately to Nathaniel in Ep 6 and publicly in Ep 7, and survives inside the ring Eldgard gave him. That's a complete arc. The ring saving him is the season's best irony about Harding protection.

**Ajax.** He's billed as "funny, patient and terrifying". So far he's only been outplayed (by Nixon's stare across the roof and by Jennie at the desk). Before Ep 8 he needs one win that costs someone.

**Chief engineer.** His dossier calls him Nathaniel's mentor, but they never share a scene, and Ep 5 plays him as the man who pays his workers. The "sold the secret, wrong about everything else" turn needs a Nathaniel scene, or the dossier should drop "mentor".

**Alice.** Her dossier says she wants Miriam's killer, but from Ep 2 to Ep 7 she believes Miriam is alive. There's no killer to want. Ep 6's "Alice's hunt reaches Grise" names a hunt nobody has defined. **Pitch:** her goal for Eps 3–6 is *who did this to my sister*. Miriam came back mute, frozen, memoryless, and Alice hunts the night she vanished. That leads her naturally to Grise, who sold the rendezvous; the stepfather comes out as a cruel deflection; and in Ep 7 "who hurt her" becomes "who killed her" in one line. It fixes the dossier and gives Alice a spine through the middle.

**The Lost One.** Her want is to free her mother, but in Eps 2–7 she only shows it in one drawing. A lead whose want is invisible for six hours reads as passive. **Pitch:** let the glimpses ("her mother's voice") do some work. Each one pulls her toward the Works physically: she wakes at the workshop window facing Tyburn; she draws the interior of the ornament because something in her knows the way home. It also answers an open problem in Ep 7, where Byron says "before the girl arrived" at Gin Lane and nothing yet says why she was heading east.
