# THE GREEN GALLOWS
## Episode 4: "The Hearing"
### Scenes 11 & 12 — Screenplay Draft


*Draft from a friend's pass, not canon. Updated to the sibling version (Hector is Tamara's brother and not yet a lord). The scene breakdown in `episodes/episode-04.md` (scenes 11–12) is the story of record.*
**CHARACTERS:**
- **THE DUKE OF WESTMORLAND (68):** Imperial titan, veteran of Inkerman, Eldgard's oldest friend. An honorable man trapped in a generational lie.
- **HECTOR LAMBRICK (34):** Heir apparent to the Lambrick coal empire, running it for his dying father. Pink-faced, genial, razor-sharp.
- **TAMARA LAMBRICK (19):** The strategist in the gallery. Hands folded, deadly.
- **ELDGARD HARDING (58):** Patriarch of House Harding. Stone in wool.
- **THE LORD CHANCELLOR (70s):** Presiding over the committee.

---

```fountain
INT. HOUSE OF LORDS - COMMITTEE ROOM A - AFTERNOON

Gothic dark oak, heavy green velvet, and leaded glass windows facing the Thames.

Overhead hangs a massive, twelve-foot chandelier of cut gallowglass crystal, humming at a low, persistent B-flat frequency that vibrates in the teeth of every peer present.

At the center witness table stands HECTOR LAMBRICK. He wears dark charcoal wool, his pink cheeks glowing with theatrical deference. In his hand, he holds two folded sheets of parchment covered in columned ink figures.

In the elevated visitor's gallery above, ELDGARD HARDING sits in the front row, upright, fingers laced over the ivory handle of his walking stick. His expression is unreadable marble.

Two seats down from him sits TAMARA LAMBRICK, wearing her out-of-season grey silk gown, a black lace fan resting lightly in her lap.

THE LORD CHANCELLOR
(adjusting his spectacles)
Mr Lambrick. The committee has indulged your preamble for twenty minutes. If you have a question regarding the Crown Charter of 1783, put it to the house.

HECTOR
(smiling with warm modesty)
Only a matter of arithmetic, my Lords. The charter grants House Harding an absolute municipal monopoly on the condition that illumination never drops below ninety-eight percent of peak candlepower across the four metropolitan districts.

Hector taps the parchment against his palm.

HECTOR (CONT'D)
On Tuesday last, output at the Tyburn vats fell to sixty-one percent. On Wednesday, fifty-nine. As we sit here in this noble chamber, the eastern mains are drawing on reserves that do not exist.

Murmurs roll through the benches.

On cue—as if summoned by Hector's words—the great green chandelier overhead HISSES.

The emerald light gutters, dipping into a muddy, jaundiced amber.

Every peer in the room tilts his head upward in unison. The sudden drop in luminescence casts grotesque, hollow shadows under their eyes.

Hector doesn't look up. He knows the timing to the second.

HECTOR (CONT'D)
The lamps themselves bear witness, my Lords. House Harding is failing.

From the front bench of the peers, a man rises.

THE DUKE OF WESTMORLAND.

Sixty-eight years old, with broad, square shoulders that carried an officer's tunic at the Alma and Inkerman. His silver hair is brushed back with military severity. On his lapel pins, among the ribbons of Crimea, glints the heavy silver star of the Order of the Garter.

The chamber falls instantly silent. When Westmorland stands, Parliament listens.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
My Lords. I have known Eldgard Harding since we were boys hunting grouse in the Berkshire scrub. I have stood inside the Works. I have seen the conduits.

The Duke speaks with the deep, effortless cadence of an imperial commander soothing a nervous battalion.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND (CONT'D)
What Mr Lambrick calls a failure is nothing more than the seasonal crystallization of the deep lines beneath the riverbed. An acoustic resonance common to every winter freeze since forty-eight. House Harding has thirty crews working the sub-mains as we speak.

The Duke glances up toward the gallery. He looks Eldgard directly in the eye.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND (CONT'D)
Within a fortnight, the lines will be purged. The light will return. London has not failed, and House Harding has not broken its word.

A palpable collective exhale sweeps the room. Several elderly peers nod, murmuring assent: *Hear, hear.*

Eldgard Harding in the gallery permits himself a fraction of an inch of relaxation in his jaw. The titan has spoken. The crisis is contained.

Hector Lambrick stands at the table, looking momentarily checked. He begins gathering his papers to sit down.

IN THE GALLERY

Tamara watches her brother falter.

Her face is completely still. Her fingers move behind the black lace of her fan.

She slides a small, stiff slip of heavy cream card out from the rib of the fan. On it, written in her neat, microscopic script, are five words:

*Ask the Duke how many days.*

She leans forward, catching her brother's eye in the lower gallery row, and lets the card drop directly into his palm as he passes to the floor.

ON THE FLOOR

The card lands in Hector's palm just as he is about to take his seat.

Hector glances down. He reads the card.

His pink face freezes. Then, slowly, the smile returns—wider, sharper, predatory.

Hector steps back up to the brass rail of the witness box.

HECTOR
My Lords. One final question for His Grace, if the committee will permit a personal inquiry.

THE LORD CHANCELLOR
Be brief, Lambrick.

Hector turns toward the Duke, his voice conversational, almost affectionate:

HECTOR
Your Grace... on Tuesday last, at the funeral of young Jamie Harding... we stood together in the vestry while the family was receiving condolences. Do you recall our conversation?

The Duke stiffens slightly.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
I spoke to many men on Tuesday, sir.

HECTOR
Indeed. But only one asked my advice on coal.
(a beat; the room goes dead silent)
You asked how much bituminous lump your sister’s estate in Kent would require to keep her conservatory and her servants' wing warm through January. You asked if my private yards could lay in thirty tons before Friday next.

The Duke’s hand rises involuntarily to his lapel, his thumb catching the serrated edge of his Inkerman medal—the medal he won when his regiment froze to death in the snow without fuel.

A terrible, suffocating quiet descends over the chamber.

HECTOR (CONT'D)
(softly, leaning across the rail)
Your Grace... if House Harding has thirty crews in the mains... and if the light will return in a fortnight... why did you order thirty tons of coal for your own blood?

The Duke does not answer.

His face drains of color, the proud military posture collapsing by half an inch.

He looks up at the gallery.

He looks at Eldgard Harding.

Eldgard does not look back.

Eldgard stares straight ahead at the royal coat of arms above the Lord Chancellor’s chair, his profile carved from black basalt. He will not meet the eyes of the man who just lied for him.

The Duke waits. One second. Two seconds. Three seconds.

He has lived sixty-eight years as an officer of the Queen and a peer of the realm. He cannot utter a flat, perjured denial under the eyes of two hundred men while his friend abandons him to the silence.

The Duke slowly removes his hand from his medal.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
(his voice hollow, stripped of thunder)
...My sister is seventy-two years old, Mr Lambrick. She feels the draft.

He does not wait for a reply.

The Duke of Westmorland turns his back on the committee and sits down in his red leather seat, his eyes fixed on the floorboards between his boots.

A broken man.

The chamber ERUPTS into shouting, banging desks, and the furious rustle of order papers.

In the gallery, Tamara Lambrick folds her lace fan with a quiet *click* and rests her gloved hands in her lap.

Beside her, Eldgard rises and walks out toward the lobby.

INT. WESTMINSTER - PEERS' ROBING ROOM - MOMENTS LATER

Heavy stone arches, cold air rising from the river terrace.

The Duke stands alone before a gothic mirror, his fingers trembling slightly as he unpins the heavy gold collar of the Garter from his shoulders.

The heavy oak door CREAKS open.

Eldgard Harding steps inside, closing the door behind him. The roar of the committee room fades to a distant murmur.

ELDGARD
You spoke well, Arthur.

The Duke doesn't turn around. He watches Eldgard's reflection in the glass.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
I spoke for Jamie. Because I held that boy in my arms when he was three days old, and because I thought his father was still a gentleman.

ELDGARD
The city cannot be told that the yield is down. You know what happens to the market if the East End panics.

The Duke finally turns, his eyes cold with ancient, wounded fury:

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
Do not lecture me on panic, Eldgard. I buried two hundred boys in the Crimea because our supply lines were a lie written on War Office paper. I know what a dying army looks like. And your house is an army that has run out of powder.

ELDGARD
It is temporary. We have the solution in hand.

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
Then keep it in your hand. Because you have used my name for the last time.

The Duke picks up his black wool overcoat and steps past Eldgard toward the river door.

ELDGARD
(a low, dangerous tone)
Arthur. If the committee votes to inspect the vaults... will you stand with House Harding?

The Duke stops with his hand on the heavy iron ring of the latch. He looks back over his shoulder, his voice quiet, devastating, and final:

DUKE OF WESTMORLAND
Ask me when the lamps in my sister's street go out.

The Duke pulls the door open and walks out into the freezing London fog, leaving Eldgard alone beneath the stone arch.

FADE OUT.
```
