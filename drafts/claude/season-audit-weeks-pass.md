# Season Audit: The Weeks Pass

A diagnostic of *The Green Gallows* as it stands on main (21 November 2026), run against the Brent Weeks narrative framework. **These are proposals, not canon.** Nothing here changes the story until the writer says so.

## How this pass is organised

The framework works at two levels, and they need different material.

- **Season level (this document):** the pillars. That means character mask and wound, the cost of power, how intelligent the antagonists are, the season's thematic question, and where the plants for each reveal go. These decisions ripple through every later episode, so they have to be run across all ten, even the ones not yet broken into scenes. If a pillar changes in Episode 1, Episode 9 changes with it.
- **Scene level (next, in order):** "yes, but / no, and" scene endings, entering late and leaving early, and trimming. These only make sense on scenes that actually exist. Right now that's Episode 2 (17 scenes), Episode 3 (partial), Episode 5 (14 scenes), and parts of 6 and 9. Episode 1's scenes are on the board.

**Recommendation:** settle the pillars below first, then run the scene pass episode by episode, starting with Episode 2. A scene pass done before the pillars are fixed would have to be redone.

## 1. Character: mask, wound, self-deception

| Character | Public mask | Private wound | Where they're flat now | What would deepen them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Nathaniel** | The cheerful, useful engineer | The spare nobody chose | Reads as too decent. His lies are justified in his head as "protecting the investigation", but the show never lets us see him believe that and be wrong. | **His blind spot is that he builds cages.** His planted genius is containment technology: the cold-sink, the device he reaches for in Ep 7. He's his father's son with a wrench. Let him notice it too late. **Tell:** he mends objects instead of feelings (the umbrella rib is already there; make it a habit). **Rationalisation to show:** "I'm hiding her because she's evidence." The truth is she's the first person who ever said "I know you." |
| **Alice** | Scrappy, unbreakable, "behind the fence" | Nobody ever stood in front of her | Strong. The risk is she's always right: she sees through Grise and stops Nathaniel. | Give her one choice the audience disagrees with that she believes is love. For example, she keeps the Lost One's first kill at the market from Nathaniel because she's protecting her, which is holding on, her lie. **Tell:** small acts of care as reflex (fastening Josephine's buttons is perfect; repeat the pattern). |
| **The Lost One** | Miriam's face | She was made into a weapon by her own mother | Risks being too innocent ("Food"). | She starts **choosing what to hide** as memories return. The folded sketch in Ep 5 is exactly this. Do more of it: she remembers something about the study and decides not to say it yet. That makes Ep 7 her choice, not a memory accident. |
| **Nixon** | The dutiful soldier | Kept, not loved | Good. He already lets Nathaniel's lie stand at the Ep 5 dinner. | Show the rationalisation on screen once: "I'm protecting Nat by not writing it down." Then he writes it down anyway. |

| **Josephine** | The composed matriarch | She *was* Miriam | Strong. | The buttons and the gloves are doing the work. Leave her alone. |

**Mirrors already in the story (lean on them):**
- Nathaniel and Nixon: two spare sons, one by birth, one by keeping.
- Alice and Jennie: the sister each lost, and the sister each could be.
- Ada and Marsh against Alice and Miriam: a sibling betrayed to protect the order, against a sibling killed for.
- **Josephine and Morgana: two mothers who used their children's lives to get what they wanted.** Josephine killed to keep her son home; Morgana sent her child into a house of captors. This is the strongest unexploited mirror in the season. They never meet, but the finale should cut them against each other.

## 2. Cost of power and the moral calculus

**The biggest Weeks problem in the season: the Lost One's cold costs her nothing.** The rules say so explicitly. In Ep 3 she kills several men and some civilians, and pays no price. Under the framework, every use of power must leave a residue.

**Proposal:** each time her cold breaks out, she **loses a piece of Miriam**. A memory goes: a street, the orange, a word in the counting game. The more she protects Alice with her power, the less of Alice's sister she has left to love her with. This:
- makes every outburst a real choice;
- gives the sisters' story a clock (how much of Miriam is left?);
- makes Alice's lie (holding on) cost something visible, since she'll want the Lost One to stop using the cold, even when it saves her;
- fits the rule that the cold "holds Miriam's body exactly as it was". Now it holds the body and spends the person.

**Other places power is free:**
- **The cold-sink.** It uses a Works crystal core: gallowglass, which is literally her mother's blood. Nathaniel is caging the daughter with the mother. Make it explicit when he learns the truth (Ep 6). It turns his best invention into an accusation.
- **The aura.** It makes Hardings comfortable. Show the cost on screen: Eldgard can't grieve Jamie properly (the Ep 2 study scene already hints at this), and Josephine, outside it, grieves fully. One line of contrast would make the rule visible.
- **The finale.** Opening the pod is framed as righteous. The framework says the protagonist mustn't get off easy. Nathaniel has to open it **knowing** people will freeze, with Nixon's frozen villages (Ep 2) and Gin Lane (Ep 6) in his head. Show at least one death the dark causes in Ep 10, not only Lambrick's victory.

**Two-bad-options choices that already work:** "I will" for the East's coal; cutting the East to keep the West lit; Alice sending the Lost One away; Nixon's second statement.

**Missing:** Alice never has to choose between the contract money and someone she loves. She took Josephine's purse in Ep 2. Make the money matter (Ren, rent, medicine), so refusing Josephine later costs her.

## 3. Plot momentum and antagonist intelligence

**Who drives the plot:** Grise (sells to everyone), Josephine (hires, kills, gives up the workshop) and Lambrick (reads the Duke, buys the location) are properly proactive. **Eldgard and Morgana are not.**

**Making Eldgard three moves ahead:**
- **Ep 1, recontextualised:** when the gauntlet misfires, Eldgard isn't contemptuous. He's afraid. He recognises the spiral as ship technology and knows what it means: 1873 is surfacing in his son. "Toys after supper" is suppression. Nobody sees it at the time; in Ep 6 it reads completely differently. This is the iceberg twist the framework asks for, and it costs nothing to plant.
- **Ep 2 already has the move:** he gives Nixon the keys and the inquiry in Nathaniel's sight. Make that deliberate. He's put a watcher on his own son.
- **Ep 5 tag (currently open):** Eldgard reads Nixon's journal. The second statement is on the page: Nathaniel hid the woman from the market. Eldgard now knows where the child he needs is likely to be, and that his son is the one hiding her. **That's the midseason tag.** It turns a quiet episode's ending into "yes, but" and puts the antagonist ahead of the hero for the back half.

**Making Morgana a player, not a battery:**
- She already sent her child to the boys in 1873, so she's a strategist in the past. Give her a move in the present.
- **Ep 4:** the lamps in the hearing chamber dip "on cue". Make it her doing. She exposes the Hardings in Parliament on purpose. The audience won't know until Ep 9, when she tells the Lost One what she's been doing with the light.

**Every thread needs its own clock:**

| Alice's contract | Grise's envelopes: a report is due every few days |

## 4. Thematic tie-in

**The season's central question, as the material now argues it:** *Is keeping someone the same as loving them?*

The framework says the magic, the politics and the protagonist's wound should all be versions of one question. They nearly are already:

| Layer | How it asks "is keeping love?" |
| --- | --- |
| **The magic** | The pod keeps Morgana alive in order to drain her. The ornament kept the child. The cold-sink keeps the Lost One's cold in, using her mother's blood. |
| **The politics** | The Hardings keep the light; keep a Hargrave close; keep the West lit by spending the East. Lambrick wants to own her. Grise keeps her girls by selling them. |
| **The wounds** | Alice holds on because nobody held her. Nathaniel wants to be kept (useful means loved). Nixon was kept instead of loved. Josephine killed to keep her son home. Ada keeps her faith. |
| **The answer the ending gives** | Alice lets go, and the Lost One stays *because* she was free to leave. Nathaniel opens the cage. Morgana lets her child stay. |

**Recommendation:** adopt this as the season's stated question, replacing "protection as control" as the headline, or sitting directly under it. It's sharper, and every scene can be tested against it: is someone keeping someone here, and is it love?

## 6. Pacing and logic

**Overload: Episode 8 is the new Episode 7.** It currently carries: Alice returns; Jennie names Josephine; the buttons; Alice confesses the contract; Josephine's lead; Nathaniel confirms the confession to Nixon; the arrest; the wedding; the ring; Tamara's Hargrave pages; Ada's "I forgave them"; Nathaniel learns about the pod; Marsh's draw-room duty; the freight plan; Ada sees the timetable; the tag. That's the same problem the writer flagged before.

**Proposed relief:** move Alice's contract confession to **Ep 6**, the night she and Nathaniel stop circling. She tells him then. It's honesty at the moment of intimacy, and it costs her with him going into Ep 7, which makes their split there even sharper. Ep 8 keeps Jennie naming Josephine, the arrest, the wedding and the pages.

**Logic checks (using the rule in `CLAUDE.md`):**
- **Ep 4, Grise's test:** Grise sends Jennie to fetch "Miriam". By then the sisters are hiding in Nathaniel's workshop. How does Jennie find her, why does the Lost One go with her, and where is Alice? This still needs a mechanism.
- **Ep 3, the market killings:** civilians die. Alice's reaction to her sister killing bystanders is missing. It's the moment her "holding on" is first tested.
- **Ep 10 is not broken yet,** but the beats show Nathaniel choosing darkness without a visible cost (see section 2).

**Scenes that may start early or end soft** (to check in the scene pass):
- Ep 2: Ren appears twice (scenes 11 and 13). Merge them.
- Ep 5's ending ("The empty chair") is quiet for a midseason. The Eldgard tag in section 3 fixes it.

## The three highest-leverage edits

1. **Give the Lost One's cold a cost: each outburst spends a piece of Miriam.** It fixes the season's biggest rule gap, puts a clock on the sisters' story, and turns every rescue into a sacrifice.
2. **Make Eldgard and Morgana play chess.** Eldgard recognises the spiral in Ep 1, and at the end of Ep 5 he reads Nixon's journal and knows his son is hiding the child. Morgana dims the hearing lamps on purpose in Ep 4. The antagonists move first in the back half.
3. **Decompress Ep 8 and plant the ending.** Move Alice's contract confession to Ep 6, and tie the frozen villages in the east to what's coming for Morgana, so the ship is set up from Ep 2.

## Questions for the writer
1. Adopt the cold's cost (a piece of Miriam each time), or a different price?
2. Do you want the season's headline question to be "Is keeping someone the same as loving them?"
3. Eldgard's Ep 5 tag (he reads the journal): yes or no?
4. The frozen villages as the first sign of the ship: yes, no, or keep it open?
5. Move Alice's contract confession to Ep 6?
